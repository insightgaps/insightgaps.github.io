Topic 3 - The Peaks and the Loop

About 6 minutes | Page 3 of 20 | All 20 topics

Houses are not built at a steady pace. Building booms when money is easy and people are hopeful, and it stops when money is tight. Preservation work follows that loop, and it rises exactly when times are hardest for owners.

Three things to take away

  1. First: A boom builds houses faster than people can fill them. New streets appear, new homes are sold, and for a while it looks as if the growth will never stop.
  2. Second: The loop turns: a boom becomes oversupply, oversupply becomes missed payments, missed payments become vacant houses, and vacant houses become preservation work. The work does not appear from nowhere — it is the other side of the boom.
  3. Third: Preservation exists because the loop is real. When you understand the loop, you understand why this work is steady, why it matters, and why it will be needed again after the next peak.
