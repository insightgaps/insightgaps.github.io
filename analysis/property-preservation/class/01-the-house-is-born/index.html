Topic 1 - The House Is Born

About 6 minutes | Page 1 of 20 | All 20 topics

A house is not a pile of bricks. It is a set of systems — structure, roof, water, wiring, heating, windows and doors — put together so that weather stays outside and people stay safe inside. Preservation begins where building ends: the day the living stops, the systems still need someone to look after them.

Three things to take away

  1. First: Every house is a set of systems, not a pile of materials. A fault in one system shows up in another: a roof that lets water in becomes a floor that rots, and a floor that rots becomes a door that will not close.
  2. Second: A house is built to be lived in and maintained. When it stands empty, nothing maintains itself — the grass grows, the water sits, the small faults become large ones. Preservation is the maintenance that continues after the living stops.
  3. Third: The first question on any property is always the same: what is this house made to do, and what is failing at that job? Answer that, and you know where to start. Answer it wrong, and you will work hard on the wrong thing.
