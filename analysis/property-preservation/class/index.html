The programme at a glance

  • Audience: university graduates, mature adults, in job training — novices in US preservation, expert learners in general. No traditional slide lectures, no beginner hand-holding.
  • Delivery system (same in every class): the trainer talks at most about 8 minutes per 30-minute session; learners talk, argue, and decide for the other 22 — except Class 1, where trainer narration runs about 10 minutes by deliberate owner order, because Class 1 IS the whole industry story.
  • One idea per class, one decision per block, one verdict per case. If a class cannot be summarised in one sentence, it has too much in it.
  • Room tools, running through every class: the Five Questions (SCOPE → FACTS → PROOF → PERMISSION → NEXT), the Update Line (from Class 2 onward, 25 words or fewer), the Decision Log (unscored), the Proof Test (from Class 3 onward: PLACE / TIME / SCOPE / CLARITY), the Money Ladder (from Class 4 onward: ALLOWABLE → BID → APPROVED → INVOICED → PAID), the Triage Card (from Class 5 onward).
  • 30-minute template (identical every class): 0:00–0:03 cold verdict on an unseen file · 0:03–0:08 cold-call defence · 0:08–0:15 Socratic pressure · 0:15–0:23 paired live audit (the foil) · 0:23–0:27 re-vote + expert reveal · 0:27–0:30 transfer bridge. Cutting rule: cut pair discussion first, then the consequence reveal. NEVER cut the re-vote + expert reveal or the transfer bridge.
  • Classroom predictions are never graded. Wrong answers are praised when reasoned; bluffing is the only failure. Plain English always; verdicts and reasoning are judged, never accent, grammar, or speed of speech. No one reads bullet points aloud, ever.
  • Scaffolding fades: Sessions 1–2, trainer models the full audit aloud, then coaches. Sessions 3–4, trainer models only the first step. Sessions 5–6, NO modelling at all — learners audit cold, defend, and get feedback only afterward.

Why this order (verified in the programme files)

  1. System first — a graduate who does not know why the work exists cannot judge it.
  2. Permission before evidence — you cannot judge whether work was done right until you know whether it was allowed at all.
  3. Evidence before money — a quantity cannot be priced until it is proven.
  4. Money before queue — judge one order completely before balancing several.
  5. Judgment last, unaided — Class 6 is the exam's rehearsal.
