Topic 5 - The Reckoning

About 6 minutes | Page 5 of 20 | All 20 topics

After a flood, everyone asks what went wrong. Who was responsible? What proof existed? What should the rules have been? That moment — when the work is judged after the fact — is called the reckoning, and it changed this industry.

Three things to take away

  1. First: A reckoning is a judgement made from records. The people who judge the work were not there when it was done; they see only what was written down and photographed. Keep your records as if they will be judged.
  2. Second: The reckoning separates what was bad luck from what was bad work. A storm is bad luck. A job done without photographs is bad work. Only proof tells the difference, and proof is the only defence you have.
  3. Third: The industry changed because of the reckoning. The rules you will meet in the next page were written by people who had seen what happens when there are no rules. Learn them as lessons, not as paperwork.
