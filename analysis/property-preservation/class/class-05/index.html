Class 5 — The Queue Under Pressure

FICTIONAL training material. Spine case #EPCS-8841 continues; the cost example below is a fictional packet figure, not a real curtailment. Property 14 Oakfield Street, Maple Heights, OH (synthetic training identifier). "Everything in this class uses a fictional client and fictional rules. Real clients differ. The skill is reading the sheet in front of you."

  • One mental model: Priority = deadlines + consequences + dependencies + the next action actually available.
  • Exit skill: Build and defend an action sequence when several orders compete.
  • Callback (60 seconds, not a retelling): Act 4, today — several clients, several rulebooks, one queue.
  • Tools in this class: the Triage Card + Effective Action Window vs Portal Due Date + the time-zone strip.

The 30-minute run

Same template as every class: cold verdict → cold-call defence → Socratic pressure → paired foil audit → re-vote + expert reveal → transfer bridge. Scaffolding in Sessions 5–6: NO modelling at all — learners audit cold, defend, and get feedback only afterward.

Lessons

  1. Due date is a portal clock; delay cost is a money clock — triage by the money clock. Sorting by portal due date alone wastes time on orders that are not urgent and starves orders that are blocked, expiring, or financially consequential.
  2. The Triage Card order: blocked ≤72 hours first, earliest due first; then unblocked ≤72 hours; then later. Ties: unsecured → missed deadline → payment risk. Priority is never reduced to money alone.
  3. Effective Action Window, not just due date: what can actually be done, by whom, in the time left? An order due in 30 minutes that needs a 2-hour action is already a different decision from an order due in 2 hours that needs 10 minutes.
  4. The loudest contractor is not the most urgent order. The foil in this class is the queue where noise and urgency are confused. Rank by the card, defend by the card.
  5. Time zones are part of triage: Dhaka = US Eastern +10 hours (summer) / +11 hours (winter); Central +11 / +12 — taught in this class, examined in the exam. A deadline read in the wrong zone is a missed deadline.
  6. Cost of delay, taught as a packet illustration: the packet uses a fictional curtailment figure as the cost of a missed blocker. It is a teaching illustration of "a missed blocker can cost more than the order was worth" — it is NOT a real curtailment, and it must never be quoted as one.

Cases (fictional, as in the packets)

  • Box 4.1: 4:45 PM, 15 minutes to shift end. The nominal portal order wastes 10 minutes; the card order does not. Learners must produce the card order and defend it.
  • Box 4.2: Orders X / Y / Z with conflicting due times, urgency, and verification states. Learners rank three orders with the card and defend the ranking.
  • Foil: the queue where the loudest contractor is not the most urgent order — same queue shape, opposite ranking once the card is applied.

Workbook / tasks

  • Pre-read (flipped): triage boxes + Alpha-1 §4 (Queue Triage & Cost-of-Delay) + one question: "What is your verdict, and what single fact decides it?" By Session 5 the pre-read is the full file with no guidance at all.
  • In class: cold verdict · cold-call defence · paired foil audit · individual Update Line (≤25 words) · re-vote.
  • Retrieval (closed-book): rank three orders with the Triage Card, in order, with one-line rationale each.
  • Decision Log: first decision, confidence 1–3, reason, what changed.

Exam preparation (what this class feeds)

  • Competency C4 (Constraint-Based Queue Prioritization): 50% sequence / 50% rationale in the exam. A correct order with no rationale scores half; a defended order with a wrong sequence is still a failure of prioritization, not a pass.
