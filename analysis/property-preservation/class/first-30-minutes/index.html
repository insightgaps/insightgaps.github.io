First 30 Minutes

Today's opening lesson. 30 minutes. Teach as professor + corporate professional + trainer: open as professor (concept + why), prove as professional (numbers + stake), practice as trainer (name her, answer, decide).

TRAINING ONLY — not a real work system. Every case, address, amount, and rule sheet here is FICTIONAL training material, unless a line is explicitly marked as real industry history. Real clients differ. The skill is reading the sheet in front of you. No client names, no real work data anywhere on this page.

SEAM — master script: this page is the opening. When both pages are used together, teach this page first, then start the today page at work order 1 and SKIP its Beginning section — do not teach history twice. Naming happens once, at entry, here.

Her name appears everywhere below as the house. Trainees protect what they named.

0–5

The Boom Babies Grow Up — and Need Houses.

A boom-era baby, born between 1946 and 1964, is starting a family in 1972. So is the house. The baby boom is why she was needed — the children of the boom grew up, formed households, and there were too few houses for them. That one link, boom babies grown up → houses, is the whole lesson in one sentence.

Ask the class: why would a country suddenly need millions of houses? Let them answer before you explain. Then use the name chosen at entry (above) and keep going.

For the teacher SAY: "A boom-era baby is starting a family in 1972. So is a house." Never say a baby born in 1972 is a baby-boomer — the boom was 1946–1964. DON'T CUT: the naming. Without a name, she is a diagram. With a name, she is theirs.

5–15

The War Ends, America Builds

The war ends. Factories convert to houses. Bretton Woods 1944 steadies world money. The Marshall Plan 1948–52 rebuilds economies. Veterans come home with savings and a shortage. The GI Bill (22 June 1944; guaranty raised to $4,000 in 1945; VA guaranteed 13.9 million loans by 1993) and FHA insurance (27 June 1934 — long loans replacing short balloon loans with 30–50% down) make a house buyable on a worker's pay. Baby boom: ~76 million births 1946–64. Levittown 1947–51: 17,000+ houses, ~$6,900–$8,000 by model. Interstates 1956. Cars: registrations 25.6 million (1945) → 61.4 million (1960).

More forces, one system — none worked alone: savings-and-loans + deposit insurance funded the loans (thrifts held ~57% of residential mortgages in the late 1960s; crisis cost taught as a range: $123.8bn taxpayer + $29.1bn industry vs $160bn auditor total; 1,043 of 3,234 thrifts failed 1986–95; FIRREA 1989 abolished FSLIC and created the RTC (Resolution Trust Corporation)); single-family zoning decided whole neighbourhoods could hold only detached houses (Berkeley 1916; Euclid 1926; 8 zoning cities in 1916 → 1,246 by 1936); utilities: farms electrified ~11% in 1935 → ~97% in 1960 (FARM/RURAL figures); suburban tracts were wired and plumbed separately by municipalities and developers; air conditioning opened the Sun Belt (share 28% in 1950 → 40% in 2000); the tax code subsidised size (mortgage deduction dates to a 1913 accident, not homeownership policy); dual incomes + the 1974 Equal Credit Opportunity Act let two paychecks count; securitisation moved mortgages into bonds (first Ginnie pass-through year differs across sources — 1968 vs 1970, flagged, not settled); houses grew (typical new house under 1,000 sq ft in 1950 → 1,660 in 1973 → 2,631 in 2017); the American Dream gave it meaning (Adams 1931: opportunity, "not a dream of motor cars and high wages merely").

The filter: redlining and restrictive covenants excluded Black families and others from the same boom. Prosperity and exclusion, side by side — never teach one without the other. White flight and school/funding sorting shaped who lived where, and which houses later fell vacant.

For the teacher SAY: "No single cause built her. Loans + zoning + funding + highways + cars + utilities + air + tax + culture built her — with a filter." DON'T CUT: the filter. A boom story without exclusion is a false story.

15–22

Two Peaks, One Loop

Keep the two peaks separate, always. TOTAL housing starts peaked in 1972: 2,379,000. SINGLE-FAMILY starts peaked in 2005: 1,716,000. The trough of 2009 was 554,000. The house is born in 1972, the biggest TOTAL building year.

Chart legend: gold bars = TOTAL starts (all homes); the green bar = SINGLE-FAMILY starts only; the grey bar = the 2009 trough (TOTAL starts at their low point after the fall — grey because it is the trough, not a peak). Never read a gold bar and a green bar as the same measure.

1972 — TOTAL starts: 2,379,000
2005 — TOTAL starts: 2,068,000
2005 — SINGLE-FAMILY starts: 1,716,000
2024 — TOTAL starts: 1,365,000
2009 — TOTAL starts (trough): 554,000

The economy loop, in four steps: jobs/income → ability to pay; interest rates → how much house the same payment buys (peak 18.63% Oct 1981; low 2.65% Jan 2021; 7.79% Oct 2023); credit standards + government programmes → who can borrow; building → supply, jobs, prices. When the loop breaks — price fall + payment shock — the family stops paying. That break is the next lesson.

For the teacher SAY: "1972 is total. 2005 is single-family. Never collapse them." DON'T CUT: the chart. Trainees remember bars, not numbers read aloud.

22–28

Where the Work Comes From

One chain moves every work order. Learn it now; it stays on the wall for the whole course.

Bankwants its money back
→
Servicermanages the loan
→
Nationalorganises the work
→
Our office does the work
→
Field crewis on site

Servicers cannot staff 50 states — that is why nationals exist, and why firms like ours exist. Every job you will learn (grass, secure, winterize, debris, inspection) travels down this chain as one work order.

For the teacher SAY: "Five links. One order travels all five." DON'T CUT: the chain. Every later lesson hangs on it.

28–30

Cliffhanger: The Family Stops Paying

The house lived, loved, and then the payments stopped. What happens to her now? Who steps in? That is the next 30 minutes — and the rest of the course.

Retrieval, before they leave: one line each — why was 1972 the peak, and name one force that built her? Do not retell. Let them tell you.

For the teacher DON'T CUT: the retrieval. If they cannot say it back, it was a lecture, not a lesson.
