Topic 10 - The Property

About 6 minutes | Page 10 of 20 | All 20 topics

Preservation is not about houses in general. It is about one house at a time — this house, on this street, with this roof and these windows. Learn to read one house properly, and you can read any house.

Three things to take away

  1. First: Houses come in kinds: a single-family home standing alone, a townhouse sharing walls with its neighbours, a single unit in a larger building. Each kind has its own entry points, its own shared walls, and its own neighbours to consider.
  2. Second: Learn to read a house from outside in: the roofline, the walls, the windows and doors, and the ground around it. The outside tells you what the inside is likely to hold — water finds the lowest point, and damage shows first where weather meets the house.
  3. Third: The single home is the unit of preservation: one house, one record, one story. Keep one record per house, and never mix two houses into one file — the day you do, the proof for both is lost.
