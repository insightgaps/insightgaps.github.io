Topic 9 - The Permission Gate

About 6 minutes | Page 9 of 20 | All 20 topics

Before any work begins, there is a gate. The order says what is allowed, and what is not allowed, and a crew must pass the gate before it starts. Work done without permission is work done at your own risk.

Three things to take away

  1. First: The gate asks three questions. Is the order real and current, or has it been changed or cancelled? Is the work described, and is it inside the scope the order allows? And does the evidence — the photographs and the notes — confirm the condition before work starts?
  2. Second: The gate protects the crew as much as the client. It is the moment you ask before you act. A crew that passes the gate can do its work knowing that someone has already agreed it is needed.
  3. Third: When the gate says no, the order goes back — with a reason. A no with a reason is better than a yes that cannot be proved. Write the reason down, so the next person does not have to guess.
