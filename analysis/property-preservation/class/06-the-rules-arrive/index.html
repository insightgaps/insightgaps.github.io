Topic 6 - The Rules Arrive

About 6 minutes | Page 6 of 20 | All 20 topics

Rules arrive to protect four things: the house, the owner, the lender and the public walking past. They are not there to slow you down. They are there because, without them, houses were left open, work was claimed that was never done, and no one could prove anything.

Three things to take away

  1. First: A rule is a lesson someone paid for. Treat it as such. When a rule says that photographs must be date-stamped and labelled before and after, it is because, once, no one could tell when a photograph was taken — or which house it showed.
  2. Second: Proof is the rule that matters most: in the eyes of the record, a job without proof did not happen. The photographs, the written record and the invoices belong in the file, in order, where the next person can find them.
  3. Third: Rules change. The paper you read today is the rule, not what someone remembers from last year. When a rule changes, your job is to learn the new one — not to argue with the old one.
