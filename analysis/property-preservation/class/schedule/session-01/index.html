The Boom timeline — diagram only

Teach (5–40) — all starting points, none removed

Teach every starting point below. No topic, no starting point, no detail area is skipped — the teacher decides how much of each to teach, not whether it exists.

War ends; factories convert. Bretton Woods 1944 steadies world money. Marshall Plan 1948–52 rebuilds economies. Veterans come home with savings + shortage: too few houses, too many families.

GI Bill (22 June 1944). Guaranty $4,000 in 1945; VA guaranteed 13.9M loans by 1993. FHA insurance (27 June 1934). Long loans replace short balloon loans with 30–50% down. Together they make a house buyable on a worker's pay.

Baby boom (~76M births, 1946–64). Levittown 1947–51 (17,000+ houses, ~$6,900–$8,000). Interstates 1956. Cars: registrations 25.6M (1945) → 61.4M (1960); household car ownership 59% (1950) → 82% (1970).

More forces, one system (within the same 5–40 block):

  • S&Ls + deposit insurance funded the loans (~57% of residential mortgages, late 1960s; 1,043 of 3,234 thrifts failed 1986–95; crisis cost taught as a RANGE, not one number — $123.8bn taxpayer + $29.1bn industry vs GAO $160bn total; FIRREA 1989 abolished the old deposit insurer and created the Resolution Trust Corporation to wind failed thrifts down).
  • Single-family zoning (Berkeley 1916; Euclid 1926; 8 cities 1916 → 1,246 by 1936; roughly three-quarters of residential land single-family-only in many large cities, approximate).
  • Utilities / electrification (farms ~11% electrified 1935 → ~97% 1960).
  • Air conditioning + Sun Belt (share 28% 1950 → 40% 2000).
  • Tax code (mortgage deduction from a 1913 accident, not homeownership policy; caps $1M in 1986, $750,000 in 2017 — VERIFY current law before teaching).
  • Dual incomes + 1974 Equal Credit Opportunity Act.
  • Securitisation (first pass-through year differs across sources — Ginnie 1968 vs 1970, FLAGGED not settled; instrument timeline: Freddie first pass-through 1971, Fannie 1981, CMO 1983, REMIC 1986).
  • House size growth (under 1,000 sq ft 1950 → 1,660 in 1973 → 2,631 in 2017; series differ — teach as a trend).
  • American Dream (Adams 1931: opportunity, "not a dream of motor cars and high wages merely").
  • 1949 Housing Act ("a decent home for every American family", 810,000 public units planned over 6 years — it took over 20 years).
  • Building technology: balloon framing from the 1830s; postwar savings came mainly from management and components, not factory housing.
  • Section 235 (1968): subsidised ownership down to 1% interest — and 10% in foreclosure by end-1973, a warning about pushing ownership without underwriting.

Say it as one system, not a list: zoning + loans + funding + highways + cars + utilities + AC + tax + culture, with the racial filter, built the American house together. No single cause built it.

The filter (40–48) — prosperity and exclusion, side by side

Redlining + restrictive covenants excluded Black families and others from the same boom. White flight + school/funding sorting shaped who lived where and which houses later fell vacant. Never teach the Boom without the filter, and never teach the filter without the Boom. The same system that built houses for some families locked other families out of them — and that sorting is part of why some of those houses later fell vacant. That sentence is the bridge to the whole course: the house the class just named was built by the Boom and will be emptied by the Fall.

Name confirmation + close (48–60)

Confirm the name chosen at entry and use it throughout — do NOT name her a second time. Her name goes on every work order from here on.

Chain map — teach it here, keep it on the wall for the whole course: Bank → Servicer → National → Our office → Field crew. One work order travels all five links. Every later session hangs on this chain: Session 5 walks one order down it, Session 9 staffs it with three roles, Session 10 judges it whole.

Retrieval (closed-book, one line each): name one force that built her, and one force that excluded. Collect both lines — the second line is the proof the filter landed.

Closing line (professor mode, one sentence): the house was born because a whole system — loans, laws, roads, cars, and wires — pointed at building her, and the same system decided, by rule and by map, who got to live in houses like her.

How to teach it — professor, corporate professional, trainer

One teacher, three modes, in this session. Professor: open with the idea, not the task — the theory, the history, the mechanism; precise terms, defined on first use (Bretton Woods, GI Bill, FHA, securitisation, thrift/S&L, zoning, REO — define each the first time it appears). Corporate professional: show the business stake in each force — what it cost, what it earned, what breaks if done wrong (the S&L range and the Section 235 warning are the stake made visible). Trainer: make them DO it — the naming vote, the 2-minute checks every 10 minutes, the closing retrieval lines; praise reasoned wrong answers, correct only bluffing and guessing; plain English always; no one reads bullet points aloud, ever.

If you run long: extend past 60 minutes; never cut the filter, the chain map, or the retrieval. If you still run long, split across two days and keep going — the session count grows, the content does not shrink.

Verify before teaching — flagged in the schedule, still flagged here

  • Tax-code caps ($1M in 1986, $750,000 in 2017): VERIFY current law before teaching.
  • First securitisation pass-through year: FLAGGED (Ginnie 1968 vs 1970) — teach the disagreement, not a settled year.
  • S&L crisis cost: teach as a RANGE ($123.8bn taxpayer + $29.1bn industry vs GAO $160bn total), never one number.
  • House-size series: series differ — teach as a trend, not a precise ladder.
  • Single-family-only land share ("roughly three-quarters in many large cities"): approximate, as stated in the schedule.
  • 1949 Housing Act: 810,000 units planned over 6 years, took over 20 years — teach the gap between plan and delivery.
