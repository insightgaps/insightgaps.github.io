Executive Summary
Question

Which service lines generate positive operating margins, and which destroy value?

Finding

Initial Grass Cut yields the highest returns (2.17× ratio), while high-volume Grass Recuts (0.71×) operate at a structural loss.

Impact

High-volume deficit lines generate a combined $2.3K loss, directly consuming margins generated by Bid Approvals (1.83×).

Action

Immediately reprice or halt Grass Recuts and Snow Removal, scale Initial Grass Cuts, and protect the Bid Approval margin engine.

Finding 01

Highest Leakage Services: Initial secure work suffers from extreme validation friction.

Evidence

Initial Secure carries a 44.4% leakage rate ($1.9K lost on 14 work orders), followed by Initial Grass Cuts at 32.8% and Snow Removal at 28.0%.

Operational Implication

Despite high headline margins, initial property secures suffer from heavy client-side rejection due to strict photo validation guidelines. Compliance checks must be reinforced at the field dispatch level.

Finding 02

Value Destroyers: Five core service types operate below the danger threshold.

Evidence

Initial Grass Cuts (2.17×), Bid Approvals (1.83×), Initial Secures (1.19×), and Bid Requests (1.14×) generate healthy margins. Conversely, Snow Removal (0.66×), Grass Recuts (0.71×), and Allowable Approvals (0.44×) represent absolute operational deficits.

Operational Implication

A ratio below 1.0× means the business pays contractors more than clients reimburse, ignoring company overhead. Continuing these services without renegotiating rates acts as a direct capital drain.

Finding 03

Strategic Imbalance: The highest-volume service line generates systemic losses.

  • Scale

    Initial Grass Cut & Bid Approval: Scale these categories immediately. They generate positive working capital and drive overall business profitability.

  • Monitor

    Initial Secure, Bid Request, Secure: Monitor closely. These service lines are profitable but carry elevated leakage rates due to photo documentation issues.

Methodology

Dataset: Based on 268 work order records spanning May to December 2025. Data source includes invoicing ledgers and client validation portals.

Calculation Logic: Income Ratio represents (Client Payments Received) ÷ (Contractor Cost). Leakage Rate represents (Total Leakage) ÷ (Invoiced Amount).

Limitations: Calculations are volume-weighted and assume fixed operational overhead holds constant across all order categories.
