Executive Summary
Question

Where does operating margin disappear, and what causes the leakage in the work order cycle?

Finding

Vendor-caused overruns dominate all other categories, accounting for 43.6% ($3.3K) of total leakage, driven by billing discrepancies and contractor-side pricing friction.

Impact

A small cohort of contractors represents the majority of cost overrun exposure, which quietly drains 17.3% of the expected operating margin.

Action

Audit the high-loss Indianapolis IN market, place underperforming contractors on immediate review, and renegotiate terms with Midwest Builders.

Finding 01

Vendor Failure Dominance: Contractor cost overruns are the primary source of profit erosion.

Evidence

Direct vendor overruns account for $3,301 (43.6%) of the total $7.6K leakage. In comparison, mixed leakage accounts for $2.0K (26.8%), documentation errors (insufficient photos) represent $1.9K (25.1%), and late submissions are minimal at $339 (4.5%).

Operational Implication

Profit loss is not primarily an administrative timing issue or a documentation issue. It is a direct vendor cost-containment failure, indicating that contract controls and estimate verification are weak.

Finding 02

Client Concentration: MCS accounts for the vast majority of margin erosion.

Evidence

ServiceLink relationships generate the most leakage at $2.1K (28.1% of the platform total), followed by FiveBrothers at $1.9K (25.7%) and MCS at $1.4K (19.0%). These three relationships represent 72.8% of all leakage.

Operational Implication

High volumes with MCS and ServiceLink compound billing friction. Narrowing the collection validation pipeline and verifying estimates with MCS represents the single highest-value recovery lever.

Additional Dimension

Geography as Friction: Florida metro regions act as high-loss centers.

Evidence

ZIP code 46202 (Indianapolis, IN) accounts for $19.8K in invoiced work and is the highest-volume cluster. Indianapolis 46201 ($14.9K) and Orlando 32801 ($13.3K) follow, whereas Midwest operations show a mixture of volume and moderate leakage.

Operational Implication

Florida's regional density creates heavy administrative and volume pressure, resulting in poorer photo documentation compliance and a higher rate of vendor overruns.

Recommended Vendor Actions
  • Review

    Immediate Review: SecureOps LLC, Lone Star Fix, and Apex Preservation must be audited immediately. Freeze new work order dispatches to SecureOps LLC.

  • Negotiate

    Renegotiate Terms: Midwest Builders has high volume ($1.0K+ total leakage) but moderate overruns. Enforce fixed-rate item pricing to cap overruns.

  • Preferred

    Preferred Vendors: GreenThumb Pros, Coastal Maintain, Sunshine Cleaning, and Texas Haulers show high photo compliance and less than 2% overrun rates. Prioritize them for new dispatches.

Methodology

Dataset: Built on 480 work order records spanning November 2025 to June 2026. Expected cost data is compared with actual payouts to isolate contractor overruns.

Calculation Logic: Vendor Leakage = (Actual Paid to Contractor) − (Expected Contractor Pay). Attributed Leakage represents all downstream uncollected revenue or write-offs on work orders completed by that contractor.

Limitations: Regional classifications are mapped by ZIP code and do not account for local contractor travel or fuel surcharge exceptions.
