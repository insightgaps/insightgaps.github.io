Executive Summary
Question

Where does operating margin disappear, and what causes the leakage in the work order cycle?

Finding

Vendor-caused overruns dominate all other categories, accounting for 69.5% ($16.7K) of total leakage, driven by billing discrepancies and QC rework.

Impact

A small cohort of contractors represents the majority of cost overrun exposure, which quietly drains 18.4% of total profit.

Action

Audit the high-loss Tampa FL market, place underperforming contractors on immediate review, and renegotiate terms with Midwest Builders.

Finding 01

Vendor Failure Dominance: Contractor cost overruns are the primary source of profit erosion.

Evidence

Direct vendor overruns account for $16,717 (69.5%) of the total $24.0K leakage. In comparison, mixed leakage accounts for $2.6K (10.7%), documentation errors (insufficient photos) represent $2.0K (8.4%), and late submissions are minimal at $378 (1.6%).

Operational Implication

Profit loss is not primarily an administrative timing issue or a documentation issue. It is a direct vendor cost-containment failure, indicating that contract controls and estimate verification are weak.

Finding 02

Client Concentration: MCS accounts for the vast majority of margin erosion.

Evidence

MCS relationships alone generate $11.0K in total leakage (45.6% of the platform total), followed by ServiceLink at $6.1K and Five Brothers at $6.0K. These three relationships represent 96% of all leakage.

Operational Implication

High volumes with MCS and ServiceLink compound billing friction. Narrowing the collection validation pipeline and verifying estimates with MCS represents the single highest-value recovery lever.

Additional Dimension

Geography as Friction: Florida metro regions act as high-loss centers.

Evidence

ZIP code 33602 (Tampa, FL) accounts for $42.6K in invoiced work and is the highest-volume cluster. Jacksonville ($34.2K) and Miami ($24.0K) follow, whereas Midwest operations (e.g. Cleveland OH at $8.8K) show much lower loss rates.

Operational Implication

Florida's regional density creates heavy administrative and volume pressure, resulting in poorer photo documentation compliance and a higher rate of vendor overruns.

Recommended Vendor Actions
  • Review

    Immediate Review: SecureOps LLC, Lone Star Fix, Ohio Property, and Clean Sweep Inc must be audited immediately. Freeze new work order dispatches to SecureOps LLC.

  • Negotiate

    Renegotiate Terms: Midwest Builders has high volume ($3K+ total leakage) but moderate overruns. Enforce fixed-rate item pricing to cap overruns.

  • Preferred

    Preferred Vendors: GreenThumb Pros, Coastal Maintain, Sunshine Cleaning, and Texas Haulers show high photo compliance and less than 2% overrun rates. Prioritize them for new dispatches.

Methodology

Dataset: Built on 268 work order records spanning May to December 2025. Expected cost data is compared with actual payouts to isolate contractor overruns.

Calculation Logic: Vendor Leakage = (Actual Paid to Contractor) − (Expected Contractor Pay). Attributed Leakage represents all downstream uncollected revenue or write-offs on work orders completed by that contractor.

Limitations: Regional classifications are mapped by ZIP code and do not account for local contractor travel or fuel surcharge exceptions.
