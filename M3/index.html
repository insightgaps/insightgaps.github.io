Step 3 of 7 in the Course Retelling • Order

Default Has an Order. Do Not Reorder It.

Why you cannot jump straight from a missed mortgage payment to a padlock on the front door.

Story Opening • October 2008

"October 2008. The automotive parts supplier in town downsizes its second shift. Mark Miller, who has lived at 742 Evergreen Road for eleven years, misses his first mortgage payment on October 1st. On November 1st, he misses payment two. A rookie contractor hears 'two missed payments' and thinks it's time to pack the van and change the locks. But default has an unbending legal order: help comes before punishment. Loss mitigation first, foreclosure second."

Figure M3.1 — The Invariant 5-Stop Foreclosure Road [FICTIONAL COMPOSITE • REGULATION X STANDARDS]

Three Lines to Remember

Memory-Bar Standard
1
"Loss mitigation FIRST, foreclosure second." Under federal law (CFPB Regulation X), a lender cannot file foreclosure before Day 120 of delinquency. Dual-tracking while reviewing an application is illegal. Help comes before punishment.
2
"Read the label before you quote the number." A default filing is a legal paper, not a repossessed house, and not a work order. In 2010, the US had 2.9 million filings, but monthly completed repossessions peaked at 102,134.
3
"Vacancy itself is damage. Empty is enough." An unheated 42-year-old house deteriorates from clogged gutters, damp eaves, and burst pipes without anyone ever throwing a brick.
