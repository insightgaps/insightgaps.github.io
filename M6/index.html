THE ORDER LIFECYCLE • 35 MINUTES ACTIVE (02:15–02:50)

Eleven Stops, One Clean Handoff: The Permission Gate

"Work order WO-4821 lands in our intake queue at 8:00 AM: Maple House, vacant secure, initial cut, forty-eight-hour clock. Before a single dollar returns from the national client, that order must travel through eleven distinct desks in unbroken sequence. One sloppy handoff, one unverified lockbox code, or one premature lock change in a lien-theory state destroys the entire file. Your job at every stop is identical: pass it on clean, or stop it honestly."

KEY POINT 1 • THE ELEVEN STOPS

Eleven Stops, In Strict Order

Order → Intake → Permission Gate → Dispatch → Field → Proof → QC → Invoice → Submission → Payment → Follow-up. One order, eleven stops, many hands. You cannot skip intake to dispatch early; you cannot submit before quality review; you cannot bill what proof cannot defend.

KEY POINT 2 • STOP 3: PERMISSION GATE

The Gate Decides Entry

Stop 3 asks the only question that matters: "Do we have the right to be here?" In Washington State under Jordan v. Nationstar, rekeying before foreclosure sale is unlawful possession. If you see signs of life—cold fridge, lit clock, recent medication—declare HOLD. Signs of life always override a client's vacant ticket.

KEY POINT 3 • THE SYSTEM WALL

Engine Prepares, Portal Receives

Never confuse the office engine with the client portal. PPW is our internal preparation engine where photos are tagged and invoices are built. The Processor audits files up to Ready, but the Processor NEVER marks RFO. The Owner or Operations Manager cross-checks and marks Ready For Office personally.
