Five Jobs, Five Standards: Done Is a Claim, Proof Is the Test

"Carlos pulls his service truck up to Maple House. In his clipboard sits a multi-service order covering grass, locks, freeze defense, debris, and condition logging. The sun is setting over the cold eaves. Carlos knows that executing the physical task with his hands is only half the battle. In property preservation, 'Done' is merely an unverified assertion. Proof is the only currency the client accepts. If the wide lot shot is missing, the grass was not cut. If the gauge needle drops, the freeze defense has failed. If the pile is not measured with tape, the debris bill is slashed. Every physical job has its own proof standard, and gaps go back—never papered over."

Key Point 01

Done Is a Claim; Proof Is the Test

In preservation, physical work completed without verified photo evidence does not exist. A grass cut without a wide lot after-shot is rejected; locks changed without keys shown in new cylinders are unpaid; an inspection without four clear elevations is worthless. Every job requires the mandatory before/during/after evidentiary triad.
Key Point 02

A Failed Test Is Information — Never Fake It

When Carlos conducts a winterization pressure test and the gauge drops 12 PSI in 15 minutes, the technician did not break the pipe. A leaking line is critical diagnostic evidence that prevents a $20,000 catastrophic flood in spring. Declaring a HOLD and escalating a plumbing repair bid protects the asset; faking the gauge is contractor fraud.
Key Point 03

Measure the Pile, Not the Guess (CY Math)

Debris removal is paid on dimensional geometry, never trailer-bed guesswork: Length × Width × Height ÷ 27 = Cubic Yards. A pile measuring $9 \times 6 \times 3\text{ ft} = 162\text{ cu ft} \div 27 = \mathbf{6.0\text{ CY}}$. Billing 10 or 15 yards because the truck was full fails the client audit immediately. Math is the evidence.
