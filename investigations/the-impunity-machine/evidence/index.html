The Impunity Machine — Evidence Appendix

This technical appendix records the methodology, audited data pipelines, published official record, and corrections log for The Impunity Machine investigation. Aggregate statistics only — zero case-level records.

Methodology and Data Audit

Primary Sources: 65 named official and established sources in the archived registry, including Police Headquarters (PHQ) Crime Statistics (2012–2024); Supreme Court of Bangladesh Annual Reports; National Forensic DNA Profiling Laboratory (NFDPL) Annual Reviews; Ministry of Women and Children Affairs (MoWCA) One-Stop Crisis Centre (OCC) Annual Reports (2001–July 2024); UN CEDAW Committee Concluding Observations (CEDAW/C/BGD/CO/8); Government of Bangladesh submissions to the UN Secretary-General (May 2024); UNFPA / Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics Violence Against Women Survey 2024; BRAC Road to Justice Study 2022; BRAC/Agile 2022; Naripokkho 2011–2018; Bangladesh Legal Aid and Services Trust (BLAST); and UN Women.

Statistical & Denominator Standards:

  • Denominator Integrity: A conviction rate without its denominator is meaningless. We publish all seven documented measurements across their explicit denominators:
    • 0.12% — Police Headquarters (2012–2017), denominator: filed cases.
    • 0.39% — Naripokkho (2011–2018, 6 districts), denominator: filed cases.
    • 0.46% — MoWCA OCC Programme (2001–July 2024), denominator: 66,711 total centre visitors (310 convictions / 66,711).
    • 0.77% — BLAST / Islam & Shaon (2003–2016), denominator: disposed court cases.
    • 0.86% — BRAC / UNDP (2009–2014), denominator: disposed court cases.
    • 2.00% — UN Women (2013–2019), denominator: filed cases.
    • 3.66% — BRAC / Agile (2021–2022), ceiling estimate, denominator: disposed accused-level defendants.
  • Disposal vs. Conviction: The CEDAW Committee figure of 53.52% is an aggregate case disposal rate (including acquittals, settlements, and dismissals), NOT a conviction rate. Conflating the two is an editorial error addressed in correction C-003.
  • Forensic DNA Bottleneck: NFDPL 2025 review documents 1,304 biological samples received and 1,204 unprocessed as of September 2025. Backlog rate: 1,204 / 1,304 = 92.33% (92%). Processed derived: 100 samples. Active stalled cases: 789.
  • Statutory Delay: WCRPA Section 20 mandates a 90-day trial completion window. BRAC's 2022 forensic legal study establishes an average trial resolution duration of 2,349 days (approx. 6.4 years), exceeding the statutory window by 26.1×.
  • Annual Caseload Accumulation: PHQ 2024 statistics document 17,544 WCRPA cases filed against 5,648 cases disposed, adding 11,896 unresolved cases to the backlog in a single calendar year.

Documented Data Voids (GA-001 to GA-012)

Twelve official datasets held or governed by state bodies are not published in open, disaggregated formats:

  1. GA-001: Annual conviction rates broken down by individual Special Tribunal and district.
  2. GA-002: Station-level First Information Report (FIR) refusal and mediation diversion rates.
  3. GA-003: Statutory 90-day trial compliance audits across all 64 districts.
  4. GA-004: National prosecution registry for Section 17 counter-lawsuits.
  5. GA-005: Complainant case withdrawal and hostile-witness abandonment logs.
  6. GA-006: Empirical comparison of pre-2020 vs post-2020 capital punishment amendment conviction rates.
  7. GA-007: District-wise turnaround and delivery timestamps for NFDPL forensic DNA profiles.
  8. GA-008: Comprehensive statutory witness and victim protection mechanism (pending enactment).
  9. GA-009: Legislative review and judicial interpretation of marital rape exceptions.
  10. GA-010: Disaggregated district-level Special Tribunal conviction data.
  11. GA-011: Cross-tabulation of court disposal outcomes (acquittal vs dismissal vs compromise).
  12. GA-012: Hospital medical examination refusal records under Section 32 mandates.
