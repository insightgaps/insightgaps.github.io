Published investigation · BD-INV-002

The Impunity Machine

A visual data investigation into Bangladesh's rape justice crisis and the institutional machinery that turns reported violence into near-zero punishment.

Published Sun Mar 01 2026 00:00:00 GMT+0000 (Coordinated Universal Time) · Revised Thu Sep 03 2026 00:00:00 GMT+0000 (Coordinated Universal Time)

In 60 seconds· Evidence in charts· The story, short· What we do not know· Method· Evidence· Right of reply· Corrections· What to watch next

In 60 seconds

  • Finding 1 (claim R-01, grade Confirmed): Only 310 convictions were recorded from 66,711 women who came through One-Stop Crisis Centres over 23 years, a 0.46% conviction rate on that denominator.
  • Finding 2 (claim R-02, grade Confirmed): Bangladesh pairs maximum legal severity with near-zero certainty of punishment: death-penalty expansion did not solve reporting attrition, backlog, evidence loss, or witness risk.
  • Finding 3 (claim R-03, grade Confirmed): Critical justice infrastructure remains thin: 14 One-Stop Crisis Centres, two national DNA machines, major unprocessed sample backlogs, and no complete national registry for key case outcomes.
  • Finding 4 (claim R-04, grade Confirmed): Forensic DNA backlog is 92%: 1,204 of 1,304 biological samples received in 2025 remained unprocessed at the National Forensic DNA Profiling Laboratory as of September 2025.
  • Finding 5 (claim R-05, grade Confirmed): The average verdict wait is 2,349 days, over six years, against a statutory 90-day tribunal limit whose compliance rate is unpublished.
  • Finding 6 (claim R-06, grade Supported): The compound probability of punishment, modelled from individually confirmed inputs, is about 0.0215%, or 1 in about 4,650, on the mid-reference model — a model, not a direct count, with a sensitivity range from 0.00034% to 0.2756%.
  • Finding 7 (claim R-07, grade Confirmed): 78% of survivors arrive after the 72-hour forensic window (BRAC 2022), while §32 of the Women and Children Repression Prevention Act mandates hospital examination — and no institution tracks hospital-refusal compliance.
  • Finding 8 (claim R-08, grade Confirmed): In 2024, new WCRPA filings (17,544) outpaced disposals (5,648) by about 3.1 times, adding 11,896 cases to the backlog in one year.

Safety rule for this report: aggregate only. No case-level tables, no identifying detail, no jigsaw identification, small-cell suppression. The live report's "Her Story" panels are a composite figure, all details drawn from confirmed primary sources, not a real individual — and this migrated report describes that composite only as a composite, never as a person.

The story, short

Scene 1: The attrition

Claim R-01. The defining query of the live report is: why does Bangladesh's rape justice system produce impunity with the consistency of a machine? The answer starts at the funnel's mouth: disclosure, police contact, charge sheet, trial, and conviction each lose most of what enters them, so a system that looks severe on paper produces almost no punishment in practice.

Key figures: 62–64% of women do not disclose non-partner sexual violence, per the UNFPA/BBS Violence Against Women Survey 2024 (27,476 women interviewed). The live report illustrates this with a composite figure — a woman aged roughly 18 to 35, living in a district without a One-Stop Crisis Centre, whose details are all drawn from confirmed primary sources. She is not a real individual.

Scene 2: Twelve measurements, one crisis

Claim R-01, continued. The table in "The evidence in charts" above is the point: do not pick the measurement that suits a headline. Each rate is correct on its own denominator, and wrong if compared as if denominators were the same.

Key conflicts, kept as ranges: 2021 rape filings cited as 6,341 (PHQ via Prothom Alo, July 2025) and 4,762 (University of Asia Pacific study) — resolved in the live report as 6,341 for 2021 and 4,762 apparently for 2022, both probable pending a primary PHQ document; March 2025 pending cases reported as 148,314 (SHARE-Net) and 151,317 (Daily Star) — reported here as approximately 150,000, with both sources cited.

Scene 3: The forensic bottleneck

Claims R-04 and R-07. DNA evidence collected at regional One-Stop Crisis Centres must be processed at the central National Forensic DNA Profiling Laboratory before reaching a specialised tribunal, while a parallel track runs through local police under Police Headquarters to submit charge sheets. If any link delays, the trial stalls — and the central link is stalled at scale.

Key figures: 1,204 of 1,304 samples unprocessed (92%), 789 cases stalled (September 2025), 2 national DNA machines; 78% of survivors arrive after the 72-hour forensic window (BRAC 2022), while §32 mandates hospital examination and no institution tracks hospital-refusal compliance.

Scene 4: The queue that never shrinks

Claim R-08. In 2024, new court filings outpaced disposals by about 3.1 times, adding nearly 12,000 cases to the queue in a single year. The queue is not a backlog that a busy year could clear; it grows by design of the system's throughput.

Key figures: Filings 17,544, disposals 5,648, net growth 11,896; average verdict wait 2,349 days; 50 of 64 districts with no full One-Stop Crisis Centre. Geography sharpens the point: Barishal has the highest intimate-partner violence rate nationally at 81.5% (UNFPA/BBS VAW Survey 2024), with one One-Stop Crisis Centre for its entire division — about nine million people, one centre, in the divisional city, a bus journey away for most residents.

Scene 5: Maximum severity, near-zero certainty

Claims R-02 and R-06. Criminological consensus, as stated in the live report, is that certainty deters crime and severity alone does not. Bangladesh chose severity: the Women and Children Repression Prevention Act was amended in October 2020 to add the death penalty for all rape, and the conviction rate did not improve, while the backlog grew. The §17 inversion illustrates the risk in the other direction: a complainant can herself be prosecuted under §17, and the live report documents a case in Cox's Bazar in which a woman who filed a rape case in March 2022 was convicted under §17 in 56 days, while her original case was still pending 1,461 or more days later as of March 2026. The 2025 Amendment Ordinance expanded §17 to include "loss of dignity or reputation", and no institution tracks §17 prosecutions nationally — only two cases are documented nationally, and the national count is unknown.

Key figures (model, not a count): Model A 0.0146 × 0.59 × 0.025 = 0.0215% (1 in about 4,650), marked Probable in live-report terms and Supported here; Model B 0.38 × 0.27 × 0.0039 = 0.0400% (1 in 2,500); Model C 0.2756% (1 in about 360); sensitivity floor 0.00034% (1 in about 294,000), ceiling 0.2756%. No single model value should be quoted without its pathway and its sensitivity range.

The live report's interventions, stated as proposals, not findings, are: redistribute One-Stop Crisis Centres to 50 or more district-level centres (36 new district centres required, prioritising Barishal, Khulna, and Chattogram); expand DNA infrastructure to at least eight fully operational regional labs with a 72-hour processing target; enact the Witness Protection Bill, drafted and pending since 2015; mandate quarterly publication of six key metrics by ministerial order; create a §17 national registry by Supreme Court administrative order; and criminalise marital rape, as required by CEDAW in 2016.

What we do not know

  • No official conviction rate exists. Bangladesh has never published an official Women and Children Repression Prevention Act conviction rate.
  • All cited rates come from independent research, NGO monitoring, and programme evaluations. Twelve independent measurements range from 0.12% to 3.66%, and the range is real — it reflects different denominators, not measurement error to be averaged away.
  • The CEDAW 53.52% figure is not a conviction rate. It is a case disposal rate — the proportion of cases receiving any verdict, including acquittals.
  • The CEDAW document uses "completion of proceedings", not "conviction". It has been repeatedly misrepresented in media as a conviction rate, and this report formally documents that error. Do not cite 53.52% as a conviction rate.
  • Twelve datasets are confirmed absent (1): annual conviction rates by tribunal and district; FIR registration rates; 90-day compliance rates; national §17 prosecution counts; withdrawal and abandonment rates; pre/post-2020 conviction comparisons.
  • Twelve datasets are confirmed absent (2): DNA processing times by district and case type; the witness protection law (drafted, pending since 2015, not enacted); the marital rape exemption (CEDAW required removal in 2016, still in force in 2026); district-level conviction data; disposal-versus-conviction breakdowns; §32 hospital-refusal tracking.
  • Each absence is held or should exist, and none is published in the form needed. Absence here is an accountability finding, not a research limitation.
  • The live report's own source registry cannot prove more than its archive: 65 entries (54 CONFIRMED, 11 PROBABLE), corrected from a published count of 91 on 2026-09-03. The full 65-entry list is in the public digest, but case-level records are withheld pending source-protection review.
  • The probability model is sensitive to its assumptions. It assumes sequential funnel stages are statistically independent, when in reality they are conditional and path-dependent, and survey inputs carry confidence intervals and self-report biases.
  • The model's direction is stable; any single point estimate is not. Punishment probability is under 0.3% across all sensitivity variants.
Method and limitations

Sources: 65 named sources in the archived registry (54 CONFIRMED, 11 PROBABLE in live-report tier language), including Police Headquarters crime statistics, Supreme Court annual reports, National Forensic DNA Profiling Laboratory reports, CEDAW Committee document CEDAW/C/BGD/CO/8, Government of Bangladesh submissions to the UN Secretary-General (May 2024), UNFPA/BBS Violence Against Women Survey 2024, BRAC 2022, BRAC/Agile, Naripokkho, BLAST, UN Women, and named investigative journalism citing primary documents (Daily Star, Prothom Alo, TBS News).

Source hierarchy, as published: (1) government official statistics, (2) UN treaty body documents, (3) peer-reviewed studies with primary data collection, (4) major NGO programme evaluations, (5) named investigative journalism citing primary documents, (6) named journalism for individual case timeline verification only, (7) international assessments citing primary data.

Tier system, as published in the live methodology: CONFIRMED means established with high confidence from primary or official sources (two or more independent primary sources, or one official source plus corroboration, or a court record, UN treaty document, or directly cited official statistic). PROBABLE means likely accurate but below the CONFIRMED threshold (one named primary source plus a corroborating pattern, or a calculation derived from two or more CONFIRMED inputs where the derivation is stated). CONFIRMED ABSENT means a data point that should exist but demonstrably does not, after active search across three or more institutional channels. This migrated report maps CONFIRMED to Confirmed, PROBABLE to Supported, and CONFIRMED ABSENT to a gap entry in "What we do not know" (with the absence itself graded Confirmed where the search is documented). The live methodology states it does not use an ALLEGED tier for any primary claim, and no claim appears without a named source.

Limitations: conviction-rate denominators differ and must never be averaged; the probability model assumes independence between funnel stages, which is a simplification; survey inputs carry confidence intervals and self-report biases; the live report's automated live-record collector (GitHub Actions, every six hours, RSS feeds classified by an AI tool, deduplicated against existing leads) assigns UNVERIFIED to all automated items, upgraded only after manual cross-reference — no automated item reaches the narrative without human verification, and this migrated report does not republish any automated lead or case record.

Public data only. No RTI, interview, or fieldwork is used in this migrated report. The live report's gaps that would require unpublished government data are stated as gaps, not filled.

What to watch next

  • Whether an official WCRPA conviction rate is ever published, and whether the twelve confirmed-absent datasets — 90-day compliance, FIR registration rates, §17 counts, DNA processing times, §32 tracking — appear in publishable form.
  • Whether the DNA backlog clears from 1,204 unprocessed of 1,304, and whether regional labs beyond the two national machines are opened.
  • Whether the Witness Protection Bill (pending since 2015) is enacted, and whether the marital rape exemption (CEDAW required removal in 2016) is removed.
