The Impunity Machine

Over twenty-three years, 66,711 women arrived at Bangladesh's One-Stop Crisis Centres. Only 310 cases concluded in a conviction. Special tribunals take an average of 2,349 days to reach a verdict against a 90-day legal limit, 92% of forensic DNA samples sit unprocessed in backlogs, and twelve vital justice datasets remain entirely uncollected.

The Missing Variable: Tanvir opens the public crisis-centre registers seeking justice for Nusrat. VIGIL plots the institutional funnel: 99.54% of cases dissolve before reaching a judicial verdict.
Decision point 1 — Decide first: would you report 0.46% of centre arrivals, or 0.12% of police FIRs?

Reveal — Claim R-01 · Confirmed. Only 310 convictions were recorded from 66,711 OCC visitors over 23 years (0.46%). Across independent studies, measured rates range from 0.12% (Police HQ filed cases) to 3.66% (BRAC disposed accused ceiling). Printing any rate without its specific denominator misleads the reader.

The Missing Variable: Tanvir inspects Nusrat's medical referral timeline. MUKTI identifies the statutory trap: the legal evidentiary clock ran out before she reached a tertiary clinic door.
Decision point 2 — Decide first: when law mandates medical evidence but provides no rapid transit, who is liable for evidence loss?

Reveal — Claim R-07 · Confirmed. Under Section 32 of the Women and Children Repression Prevention Act, forensic medical examination is mandatory. Yet 78% of survivors arrive after 72 hours (BRAC 2022). No state agency tracks hospital-refusal compliance or provides emergency transit.

Screen 3 · Forensic Bottleneck Audit

The DNA Backlog: 92% Unprocessed

92.3% UNTESTED 1,204 of 1,304 biological samples stalled · 2 national machines
92% UNPROCESSED Two national machines serve 170 million people; 1,204 rape kits gather dust untested in 2025.
The Missing Variable: VIGIL traces the laboratory queue. Nusrat's evidentiary swab sits locked in refrigerated storage: two government DNA analyzers handle evidence for the entire country.
Decision point 3 — Decide first: is a 92% DNA backlog an equipment shortage, or an institutional evidentiary graveyard?

Reveal — Claims R-03 & R-04 · Confirmed. Critical justice infrastructure is thin: 14 One-Stop Crisis Centres across 64 districts and two national DNA machines. In 2025, 1,204 of 1,304 received samples (92.3%) remained unprocessed, leaving at least 789 active court trials indefinitely stalled.

Screen 4 · Special Tribunal Caseflow Audit

The Wait: 2,349 Days Against 90-Day Limit

2,349 DAYS 6.4+ years average wait · Filings outpace disposals 3.1×
2,349-DAY WAIT Statutory trial limit is 90 days. Actual verdicts take 2,349 days—over six grueling years.
The Missing Variable: Nusrat's file enters a Special Tribunal queue. MUKTI computes the human cost: a six-year delay is not administrative inertia; it is judicial attrition engineered to fatigue complainants.
Decision point 4 — Decide first: in a six-year trial, which party breaks first: the survivor or the bailed defendant?

Reveal — Claims R-05 & R-08 · Confirmed. The average wait for a tribunal verdict is 2,349 days (over 6.4 years), exceeding the 90-day statutory limit by 26 times. In 2024, new filings (17,544) outpaced disposals (5,648) by 3.1 times, adding 11,896 backlogged cases in a single year.

Screen 5 · Criminal Deterrence Audit

The Illusion: Maximum Severity, Near-Zero Certainty

1 IN 4,650 Compound probability of punishment: 0.0215% (range 0.00034%–0.2756%)
1 IN 4,650 CERTAINTY Capital punishment expands statutory severity, yet compound probability of facing penalty remains 1 in 4,650.
The Missing Variable: Tanvir reads the statute: mandatory death penalty. VIGIL models the statistical probability: near-absolute impunity at every preceding institutional filter.
Decision point 5 — Decide first: does expanding the death penalty deter crime when conviction certainty is near zero?

Reveal — Claims R-02 & R-06 · Confirmed & Supported. Expanding capital punishment in 2020 inverted criminological deterrence. The compound probability of punishment is 0.0215% (1 in 4,650) on the mid-reference model, with a sensitivity range of 0.00034% to 0.2756%.

The Impunity Machine in Five Lines

  1. 66,711 women entered state crisis centres; only 310 convictions were ever logged.
  2. 78% of victims arrive after the critical 72-hour physical evidence collection window.
  3. Over 92% of biological DNA samples remain unprocessed in state laboratories.
  4. Tribunal trials take 2,349 days on average, while new cases outpace disposals 3.1 to 1.
  5. The legal system promises maximum penalty but delivers a 1-in-4,650 chance of punishment.

Documented Gaps & Disclosures
