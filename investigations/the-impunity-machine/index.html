The Impunity Machine in Five Lines

  1. 66,711 women entered state crisis centres; only 310 convictions were ever logged.
  2. 78% of victims arrive after the critical 72-hour physical evidence collection window.
  3. Over 92% of biological DNA samples remain unprocessed in state laboratories.
  4. Tribunal trials take 2,349 days on average, while new cases outpace disposals 3.1 to 1.
  5. The legal system promises maximum penalty but delivers a 1-in-4,650 chance of punishment.

The Verdict Handed to the Reader

Is Bangladesh's gender justice failure an underfunded legal system, or an impunity machine operating exactly as designed?

Decide-First Inquiries & Empirical Findings

1. Denominator Standard: Only 310 convictions were recorded from 66,711 OCC visitors over 23 years (0.46%). Across independent studies, measured rates range from 0.12% (Police HQ filed cases) to 3.66% (BRAC disposed accused ceiling). Printing any rate without its specific denominator misleads the reader.

2. Forensic Timing Gap: Section 32 WCRPA mandates hospital medical exam, yet 78% of survivors arrive after 72 hours (BRAC 2022). No agency tracks hospital-refusal compliance.

3. Laboratory Bottleneck: Critical justice infrastructure remains thin: 14 OCCs across 64 districts and two national DNA machines. In 2025, 1,204 of 1,304 received samples (92.3%) remained unprocessed, leaving at least 789 active court trials indefinitely stalled.

4. Trial Duration: Average wait for a tribunal verdict is 2,349 days (over 6.4 years), exceeding the 90-day statutory limit by 26 times. In 2024, new filings (17,544) outpaced disposals (5,648) by 3.1 times, adding 11,896 backlogged cases in a single year.

5. Deterrence Inversion: Expanding capital punishment in 2020 inverted criminological deterrence. The compound probability of punishment is 0.0215% (1 in 4,650) on the mid-reference model, with a sensitivity range of 0.00034% to 0.2756%.

Documented Gaps & Twelve Absent Datasets

The twelve confirmed absent datasets (GA-001 to GA-012) include unpublished statutory compliance audits, untracked emergency medical refusal logs, and missing national registries:

  1. GA-001: Annual conviction rates broken down by individual Special Tribunal and district.
  2. GA-002: Station-level First Information Report (FIR) refusal and mediation diversion rates.
  3. GA-003: Statutory 90-day trial compliance audits across all 64 districts.
  4. GA-004: National prosecution registry for Section 17 counter-lawsuits.
  5. GA-005: Complainant case withdrawal and hostile-witness abandonment logs.
  6. GA-006: Empirical comparison of pre-2020 vs post-2020 capital punishment amendment conviction rates.
  7. GA-007: District-wise turnaround and delivery timestamps for NFDPL forensic DNA profiles.
  8. GA-008: Comprehensive statutory witness and victim protection mechanism (pending enactment).
  9. GA-009: Legislative review and judicial interpretation of marital rape exceptions.
  10. GA-010: Disaggregated district-level Special Tribunal conviction data.
  11. GA-011: Cross-tabulation of court disposal outcomes (acquittal vs dismissal vs compromise).
  12. GA-012: Hospital medical examination refusal records under Section 32 mandates.
