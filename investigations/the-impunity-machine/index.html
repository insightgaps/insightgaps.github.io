The Impunity Machine

Over twenty-three years, 66,711 women arrived at Bangladesh's One-Stop Crisis Centres. Only 310 cases concluded in a conviction. Special tribunals take an average of 2,349 days to reach a verdict against a 90-day legal limit, 92% of forensic DNA samples sit unprocessed in backlogs, and twelve vital justice datasets remain entirely uncollected.

Step 1 · The Rates & Denominators

Seven Measurements, Zero High Conviction Rates

Across twenty-three years (2001 to July 2024), 66,711 survivors of violence arrived at One-Stop Crisis Centres (OCC) across Bangladesh. Exactly 310 cases concluded in a conviction — a conviction rate of 0.46% on the denominator of centre visitors, not cases filed or decided. Published studies by police, legal aid organisations, and international bodies yield seven distinct conviction measurements, spanning from 0.12% (Police Headquarters filed cases) to 3.66% (BRAC/Agile ceiling on disposed cases). None exceeds 3.7%, and Bangladesh has never published an official conviction rate for this law.

Crucially, the government's CEDAW submission reports a 53.52% "disposal rate." Disposal measures case closure (including dismissals, compromises, and acquittals), not convictions. Conflating disposal with conviction is an egregious statistical fallacy.

Key Finding (Claim R-01 · Confirmed): 310 convictions from 66,711 OCC arrivals (0.46%). True conviction rates range from 0.12% to 3.66% across distinct denominators. CEDAW 53.52% is disposal, not conviction.

Step 2 · The Funnel

The Justice Funnel: Where Cases Disappear

The path to justice narrows at the very first step. According to the UNFPA/BBS National Violence Against Women Survey 2024 (27,476 women interviewed), 62% to 64% of women do not disclose non-partner sexual violence. After disclosure come police contact, a charge sheet, a trial, and finally a conviction — each stage losing most of what entered it. This investigation gives you no rate for each step, because the published record supplies no single clean set of stage rates, and inventing them would be worse than leaving them blank. What it does supply is the two ends of the journey: tens of thousands of arrivals at one end, 310 convictions at the other, and rates in Step 1 that all land below 4% on any denominator. A system that looks severe on paper produces almost no punishment in practice, and the funnel is where that severity leaks away.

Key Finding (Claim R-01 · Confirmed): 62%–64% of survivors never disclose. The system loses cases at every operational stage before trial.

Step 3 · Severity vs. Certainty

Inverting Deterrence: Capital Punishment with Zero Certainty

In October 2020, following nationwide public protests, Bangladesh amended the Women and Children Repression Prevention Act (WCRPA) to introduce the death penalty for rape. Criminological science establishes that deterrence depends on the certainty of punishment, not its severity. Enacting maximum statutory severity while conviction rates remain near zero creates symbolic legislation without deterrent effect.

Key Finding (Claim R-02 · Confirmed): The 2020 capital amendment increased penalty severity while certainty remained under 1%, inverting classical deterrence principles.

Step 4 · Crisis Centres

50 of 64 Districts Lack a Full Crisis Centre

Specialized institutional support is severely centralized. Bangladesh operates full multi-sectoral One-Stop Crisis Centres in only 14 government medical college hospitals across the country. In 50 of 64 districts (78.1%), survivors have no full OCC facility providing simultaneous emergency medical treatment, forensic documentation, legal counsel, and police support. While 47 regional crisis cells exist, they lack full forensic and legal capacities.

Key Finding (Claim R-03 · Confirmed): 50 of 64 districts lack a full OCC. Barishal records the highest intimate-partner violence at 81.5% (UNFPA/BBS 2024).

Step 5 · 72-Hour Medical Window

78% of Survivors Arrive After Evidence Degrades

Forensic physical evidence and biological DNA degrade rapidly following an assault. Medical protocols require forensic examinations within 72 hours. However, empirical studies reveal that 78% of survivors reach a hospital facility after the 72-hour window has expired (BRAC 2022). While Section 32 of the WCRPA mandates government hospitals to conduct examinations, authorities maintain zero public records tracking hospital examination denials.

Key Finding (Claim R-07 · Confirmed): 78% of survivors arrive after 72 hours (BRAC 2022). 22% arrive within the window (derived: 100 − 78). Hospital refusal tracking is absent (GA-012).

Step 6 · DNA Bottleneck

92% of DNA Samples Unprocessed

Even when biological evidence is collected, processing is bottlenecked at the central level. All forensic DNA samples collected nationwide must be processed by the National Forensic DNA Profiling Laboratory in Dhaka. In 2025, out of 1,304 samples received, 1,204 remained unprocessed as of September 2025 — a backlog rate of 92.3% (published as 92%). Only 100 samples (7.7%, rounded to 8%) were completed. Simultaneously, 789 cases were stalled in court awaiting forensic reports.

Key Finding (Claim R-04 · Confirmed): 92% of DNA samples (1,204 of 1,304) unprocessed in Sep 2025. 100 processed (8%, derived). 789 cases stalled.

Step 7 · The 2,349-Day Wait

Statutory 90-Day Limit vs. Six-Year Trial Delays

Under Section 20 of the WCRPA, special tribunals are legally mandated to conclude trials within 90 days. In reality, a field audit of 385 completed cases across 16 districts revealed an average verdict wait of 2,349 days — over 6.4 years (BRAC 2022). Trials take 26.1 times longer than the statutory limit. The official rate of 90-day compliance is unmeasured and unpublished by the judicial system.

Key Finding (Claim R-05 · Confirmed): Average verdict wait is 2,349 days (6.4+ years) vs. the 90-day statutory limit (2,349 ÷ 90 = 26.1× delay). Compliance rate is unpublished.

Step 8 · Case Backlog

Filings Outpace Disposals by 3.1×

The judicial queue grows through structural throughput deficits. In 2024, 17,544 new cases were filed under the WCRPA while only 5,648 cases were disposed of, adding 11,896 cases to the backlog in a single year. Filings outpace tribunal disposals by 3.1 times (17,544 ÷ 5,648 = 3.11×). Total pending caseload in special tribunals stands at approximately 150,000 cases (148,314 SHARE-Net; 151,317 Daily Star).

Key Finding (Claim R-08 · Confirmed): In 2024, 17,544 new filings vs. 5,648 disposals (+11,896 net backlog growth; 3.1× ratio). Cumulative backlog ~150,000 cases.

Step 9 · Deterrence Model

The Statistical Probability of Punishment

Combining the probabilities of disclosure (36%), arrest/FIR filing, prosecution, and final conviction produces an empirical punishment model. The probability of an offender facing legal punishment ranges from a conservative floor of 0.00034% (3.4 per million offenses) to a midpoint of 0.0215% (2.1 per ten thousand offenses), reaching a ceiling of 0.2756%. The mathematical likelihood of conviction is statistically negligible.

Key Finding (Claim R-06 · Supported): Modelled probability of punishment: floor 0.00034%, mid 0.0215%, ceiling 0.2756%. Criminological deterrence has collapsed.

Step 10 · Absent Datasets

Twelve Confirmed Institutional Voids

Institutional impunity is sustained by missing records. The bureau confirmed twelve critical datasets (GA-001 through GA-012) that are absent from public releases across rates and compliance, legal protections, and forensic operations. These include annual conviction rates by district tribunal, FIR registration refusal logs, 90-day statutory compliance tracking, false-allegation (§17) prosecution metrics, and district-level DNA processing times.

Key Finding (Claim R-05 · Confirmed): Twelve key justice datasets are confirmed absent from official records (GA-001 to GA-012).

Documented Gaps & Counting Clarifications

  • Seven Measurements Reconciled: Earlier drafts referenced "twelve measurements"; the verified record supplies seven independent conviction rates (0.12% to 3.66%) plus the CEDAW disposal rate (53.52%). The twelve absent datasets (GA-001 to GA-012) remain fully confirmed.
  • Stalled Cases vs. Unprocessed DNA: 789 court cases stalled awaiting DNA is reported alongside 1,204 unprocessed samples; the public record does not specify whether the 789 cases represent a subset of the 1,204 samples. Both are published separately without conflation.
  • 90-Day Compliance: While the 2,349-day average wait is audited, the nationwide proportion of cases concluding within 90 days remains unpublished by judicial authorities.
