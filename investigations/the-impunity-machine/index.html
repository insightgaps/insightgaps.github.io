The Impunity Machine

A visual data investigation into Bangladesh's rape justice crisis and the institutional machinery that turns reported violence into near-zero punishment.

The story in visuals

Step 1 · Claim R-01 · Confirmed

Twelve measurements, one crisis

Only 310 convictions were recorded from 66,711 women who came through One-Stop Crisis Centres over 23 years — 0.46% on that denominator. No official WCRPA conviction rate has ever been published.

The CEDAW 53.52% figure is a disposal rate, not a conviction rate, and must never be cited as one. Correction C-003 added that denominator note.

Step 2 · Claim R-01 · Confirmed

The funnel loses most of what enters it

Disclosure, police contact, charge sheet, trial, and conviction each lose most of what enters them. A system that looks severe on paper produces almost no punishment in practice.

62–64% of women do not disclose non-partner sexual violence, per the UNFPA/BBS Violence Against Women Survey 2024 (27,476 women interviewed).

Step 3 · Claim R-04 · Confirmed

DNA waits, trials stall

DNA evidence from regional centres must be processed at the central National Forensic DNA Profiling Laboratory before reaching a specialised tribunal. That central link is stalled at scale.

78% of survivors arrive after the 72-hour forensic window (BRAC 2022), while §32 mandates hospital examination — and no institution tracks hospital-refusal compliance. Claim R-07, Confirmed.

Step 4 · Claim R-03 · Confirmed

Critical infrastructure stays thin

14 One-Stop Crisis Centres, two national DNA machines, major unprocessed sample backlogs, and no complete national registry for key case outcomes.

Barishal has the highest intimate-partner violence rate nationally at 81.5% (UNFPA/BBS VAW Survey 2024), with one centre for its entire division — about nine million people, one centre, in the divisional city, a bus journey away for most residents.

Step 5 · Claim R-08 · Confirmed

The queue that never shrinks

In 2024, new filings outpaced disposals by about 3.1 times, adding 11,896 cases in one year. The average verdict wait is 2,349 days — over six years — against a statutory 90-day tribunal limit whose compliance rate is unpublished. Claim R-05, Confirmed.

Step 6 · Claim R-06 · Supported

Maximum severity, near-zero certainty

The compound probability of punishment, modelled from individually confirmed inputs, is about 0.0215% on the mid-reference model. The model assumes sequential stages are independent, when in reality they are conditional — so its direction is stable, but no single point estimate should be quoted without its pathway and range.

Bangladesh chose severity: the WCRPA was amended in October 2020 to add the death penalty for all rape, and the conviction rate did not improve, while the backlog grew. Claim R-02, Confirmed.

Absent · Rates and compliance

Annual conviction rates by tribunal and district; FIR registration rates; 90-day compliance rates; withdrawal and abandonment rates; pre/post-2020 conviction comparisons.

Absent · Protection and law

National §17 prosecution counts; witness protection law (drafted, pending since 2015, not enacted); marital rape exemption (CEDAW required removal in 2016, still in force in 2026).

Absent · Forensic tracking

DNA processing times by district and case type; district-level conviction data; disposal-versus-conviction breakdowns; §32 hospital-refusal tracking.

Absent · What absence means

Each absence is held or should exist, and none is published in the form needed. Absence here is an accountability finding, not a research limitation.

Step 7 · Twelve confirmed-absent datasets

What the system does not publish is part of the story

Twelve datasets are confirmed absent (GA-001 to GA-012 in the methodology). The full list is in the evidence appendix.

Proposals in the live report, not findings: 50 or more district-level centres; at least eight regional DNA labs with a 72-hour target; enact the Witness Protection Bill; mandate quarterly publication of six key metrics; create a §17 national registry; criminalise marital rape.

What we do not know

  • No official conviction rate exists. Bangladesh has never published an official WCRPA conviction rate.
  • All cited rates come from independent research, NGO monitoring, and programme evaluations. Twelve independent measurements range from 0.12% to 3.66%, and the range is real — it reflects different denominators, not measurement error to be averaged away.
  • The CEDAW 53.52% figure is not a conviction rate. It is a case disposal rate — the proportion of cases receiving any verdict, including acquittals.
  • The CEDAW document uses "completion of proceedings", not "conviction". It has been repeatedly misrepresented in media as a conviction rate, and this report formally documents that error.
  • Twelve datasets are confirmed absent. See Step 7 and the appendix for the full list.
  • The live report's own source registry cannot prove more than its archive: 65 entries (54 CONFIRMED, 11 PROBABLE), corrected from a published count of 91 on 2026-09-03. Case-level records are withheld pending source-protection review.
  • The probability model is sensitive to its assumptions. It assumes sequential funnel stages are statistically independent, when in reality they are conditional and path-dependent, and survey inputs carry confidence intervals and self-report biases.
  • The model's direction is stable; any single point estimate is not. Punishment probability is under 0.3% across all sensitivity variants.

What to watch next

  • Whether an official WCRPA conviction rate is ever published, and whether the twelve confirmed-absent datasets appear in publishable form.
  • Whether the DNA backlog clears from 1,204 unprocessed of 1,304, and whether regional labs beyond the two national machines are opened.
  • Whether the Witness Protection Bill (pending since 2015) is enacted, and whether the marital rape exemption (CEDAW required removal in 2016) is removed.
  • Whether a fresh, logged reply attempt is made before any new allegation beyond what is already published — no reply is recorded in the appendix table.
