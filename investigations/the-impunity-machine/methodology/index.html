Methodology: The Impunity Machine

On this page: In short · Evidence tiers · What we do not know · Conviction-rate methodology · Probability models · Method and limitations · Further method detail · Evidence · Right of reply · Corrections

In short

  • Tiers: CONFIRMED maps to Confirmed, PROBABLE to Supported, CONFIRMED ABSENT to a gap entry. No ALLEGED tier is used for any primary claim, and no claim appears without a named source.
  • Conviction rates: 0.12% to 3.66% across seven measurements on different denominators; CEDAW 53.52% is a disposal rate, never a conviction rate.
  • Probability of punishment: Model A 0.0215%, Model B 0.0400%, Model C 0.2756%; sensitivity floor 0.00034%, ceiling 0.2756% — under 0.3% across all variants.
  • Source hierarchy: government statistics first, then UN treaty documents, peer-reviewed studies, NGO programme evaluations, named journalism citing primary documents, journalism for case-timeline verification only, and international assessments citing primary data.

Evidence tier system

Every factual claim in BD-INV-002 carries one of three evidence tier labels in the live methodology. These are formal classifications, not editorial judgments.

Tier Definition Rule for assignment
CONFIRMED Established with high confidence from primary or official sources Two or more independent primary sources; or one official source plus corroborating evidence; or a court record, UN treaty body document, or directly cited official statistic with an accessible URL
PROBABLE Likely accurate but below the CONFIRMED threshold One named primary source plus a corroborating pattern from CONFIRMED data; or a calculation derived from two or more CONFIRMED inputs where the derivation is stated, including modelled estimates where inputs are CONFIRMED
CONFIRMED ABSENT A data point that should exist but demonstrably does not Active search across three or more institutional channels returns no result; the absence is an accountability finding

This investigation does not use an ALLEGED tier for any primary claim in its methodology. No claim appears without a named source. In this site's new grade system, CONFIRMED maps to Confirmed, PROBABLE maps to Supported, and CONFIRMED ABSENT maps to a gap entry in "What we do not know", with the absence itself graded Confirmed where the search is documented.

What we do not know

  • No official WCRPA conviction rate has ever been published. The twelve measurements below are the published range, and the range itself is the finding.

Probability of punishment model

Model A, incident-to-verdict funnel (mid-reference): 0.0146 × 0.59 × 0.025 = 0.0215% (1 in about 4,650). Inputs: reporting 1.46%, charge sheet 59% (PBI 2016–2023), conviction at trial 2.5% (specialised tribunal average).

Model B, survey disclosure pathway: 0.38 × 0.27 × 0.0039 = 0.0400% (1 in 2,500). Inputs: disclosure 38% (UNFPA 2024), police contact 27% (UNFPA 2024), conviction on filed cases 0.39% (Naripokkho).

Model C, trial-centric pathway: 0.38 × 0.27 × 0.59 × 0.487 × 0.0935 = 0.2756% (1 in about 360). Inputs add trial start 48.7% and conviction on resolved cases 9.35% (both BRAC 2022).

Sensitivity: floor 0.00034% (1 in about 294,000), ceiling 0.2756% (1 in about 360). Across all variants, the compound probability remains under 0.3%. Limitations: the model assumes sequential stages are statistically independent, when they are conditional and path-dependent; denominator stacking must be avoided (do not multiply a pre-trial rate by a filed-case conviction rate that already incorporates pre-trial attrition); survey inputs carry confidence intervals and self-report biases.

Method and limitations

Source hierarchy: (1) official government statistics, (2) UN treaty body documents, (3) peer-reviewed studies with primary data collection, (4) major NGO programme evaluations, (5) named investigative journalism citing primary documents, (6) named journalism for individual case timeline verification only, (7) international assessments citing primary data.

Documented source conflicts: 2021 rape filings, 6,341 (PHQ via Prothom Alo, July 2025) versus 4,762 (University of Asia Pacific study) — resolved as 6,341 for 2021, 4,762 apparently for 2022, both probable pending a primary PHQ document. March 2025 pending cases, 148,314 (SHARE-Net) versus 151,317 (Daily Star) — reported as approximately 150,000, both confirmed, difference likely from different administrative cutoff dates.

Further method detail — visual and live-record method

Visual methodology, in brief: the dot matrix represents an estimated annual non-partner violence cohort (probable, bureau model), with attrition percentages from UNFPA 2024 and PBI data; the clock animation compares 56 days (a §17 case, arrest to sentencing, confirmed) with 2,349 days (BRAC/Agile average verdict time); the race bars use a square-root scale, labelled explicitly, because at linear scale the Bangladesh bar would be less than one pixel wide; the division map uses UNFPA/BBS VAW Survey 2024 rates, corrected from version 1.0; the severity scatter uses an ordinal, not interval, severity axis.

Live record methodology, as published: a GitHub Actions collector runs every six hours, scrapes named RSS feeds, classifies items with an AI tool, deduplicates against existing leads, and appends to a leads file. All automated items start as UNVERIFIED and are upgraded only after manual cross-reference. No automated item reaches the narrative without human verification. This migrated site does not republish any automated lead or case record.
