Published investigation · BD-INV-003

The Lead Belt

Bangladesh's lead poisoning crisis mapped: 294 contaminated sites, 145 school–site intersections (121 unique schools) within 500 metres, and soil lead readings up to 1,702 times the international safety standard.

Published Mon May 25 2026 06:00:00 GMT+0600 (Bangladesh Standard Time) · Revised Thu Sep 03 2026 06:00:00 GMT+0600 (Bangladesh Standard Time)

The story in visuals

Step 1 · Claims R-02, R-03 · Confirmed

59 metres from a school gate

In Rosulpur, Kamrangir Char, Dhaka, a battery recycling factory operates adjacent to primary school grounds. A child walks through the school gate each morning; the soil between the gate and the factory holds that lead, and lead does not biodegrade.

Key figures: 59 m, school gate to factory (BD-4591); soil lead 330,001 ppm — 825 times the US EPA residential threshold; 18 schools within 500 metres of this single furnace.

Step 2 · Claim R-03 · Confirmed

The highest reading is 1,702 times safe

The highest soil lead reading in the report reaches 680,872 ppm at Kathgora, Savar, about 1,702 times the 400 ppm international residential safety threshold.

Across Bangladesh, 68 sites are designated critical zones, with 65 exceeding 100,000 ppm (250 times safe). The two numbers are different and must not be merged. Claims R-08, Confirmed.

Step 3 · Claim R-02 · Confirmed

294 sites, 145 intersections, 121 unique schools

294 contaminated sites are mapped across Bangladesh using Pure Earth and University of Dhaka assessment data, across 93 upazilas in all divisions. Claim R-01, Confirmed.

145 school–site intersections place schools within 500 metres of contaminated sites, for the 44 formally assessed headline sites. The unique-school count is 121, and 24 overlap instances explain the difference.

Step 4 · Claim R-06 · Confirmed

A national burden, uneven by place

Lead causes permanent cognitive deficit and organ damage. In MICS 2025, 38.3% of children nationally and 65.2% in Dhaka Division exceed safe blood-lead limits, with Rajshahi lowest at 15.9%.

Do not quote clinical cohorts as population rates: 98% of 500 tested Dhaka toddlers aged 2–4 exceeded CDC reference standards in a clinical cohort (Sultana et al., 2026) — never a national or divisional rate.

Step 5 · Claim R-07 · Confirmed

Formal versus informal: 6 against 6,000

Only six formal, licensed recyclers operate in Bangladesh, processing about 30% of national battery waste. More than 6,000 informal, unregistered workshops process about 70% without emission scrubbers.

In 2025, the Department of Environment shut down 28 facilities in Jatrabari. A closure is an enforcement event; continued visual activity after a closure is a separate finding, and neither is remediation.

Exposure, projected — not counted

About 39,875 exposure instances projected (145 intersections × 275 average enrolment), unique-school estimate about 33,275, audited range 24,650–50,750. Claim R-05, Supported. Never quote it as "39,875 children poisoned".

Remediation, zero

Zero contaminated sites have been permanently remediated or cleaned up, on the public record at the coordinates analysed. Claim R-04, Confirmed. Enforcement closures and demolitions are not remediation.

Modelled toll, labelled as modelled

Lancet Planetary Health 2023 estimates 138,054 cardiovascular deaths and $28.63 billion annual economic toll, about 6% of GDP — a modelled estimate, not a counted death toll.

Satellite, corroboration only

Satellite verification in March 2026 confirmed visual indicators of continued activity at Site BD-4802, 351 days after the crackdown, while BD-4591 was never targeted in that drive. Visual indicators, not proof of operation.

Step 6 · Claims R-04, R-05, R-08 · Confirmed / Supported

22 years of documentation, zero permanent cleanups

The timeline, in short: 2003 — BBS documents 12,207 battery recycling operations; 2019 — Pure Earth and University of Dhaka assess 270+ sites, no permanent closures follow; 2021 — Kathgora blood-lead evidence at an abandoned site; February 2025 — first formal MoU in 22 years; April 2025 — 28 sites closed in Jatrabari and Savar, BD-4591 untouched; November 2025 — MICS 2025 confirms the divisional gap; March 2026 — this analysis publishes.

The full 11-entry timeline, with every dated event, is in the evidence appendix. Demands in the live report, stated as proposals not findings: publish the full certificate registry; inspect or serve closure orders on all 44 school-adjacent sites within 90 days; commission a national soil remediation programme within one year, prioritising the 18 schools near BD-4591.

What we do not know

  • The published and reconstructed counts differ, and both are shown. Published: 44 sites with schools, 145 intersections, 121 unique schools for the headline sites.
  • Reconstructed counts: 51 sites, 166 intersections, 125 unique schools across all 294 sites in the provenance note; the methodology page also reports 132 unique schools and 166 intersections when counting schools newly mapped in OpenStreetMap.
  • The discrepancy is due to OpenStreetMap database drift between the original extraction window and the archived snapshot. It was not silently normalised: the published figures are retained as the publication of record.
  • The master dataset is not in the repository. The live investigation metadata references a Lead Belt master dataset v5 in Excel and CSV, with status "not-in-repository", and notes restoration is pending an owner decision.
  • Per-site values cannot be independently re-run from this draft alone. The spatial replication script (methods/analyze.py in the live repository) is referenced, not republished here, and that is a gap.
  • Exposure instances are not tested children. The 39,875 figure multiplies intersections by an average enrolment of 275 students per school (Annual Primary School Census 2024 / Directorate of Primary Education).
  • 39,875 must never be quoted as "39,875 children poisoned" or "children at extreme risk" — the 2026-09-03 correction reframed it exactly as a projection of exposure instances.
  • Historical blood-lead figures are not current figures. A 2000 Dhaka study (779 children, mean 15.0 µg/dL) belongs to the leaded-petrol era and must never be presented as current.
  • Current divisional figures in this report are MICS 2025, and clinical cohort figures are labelled by study, year, and sample.
  • Satellite activity indicators are visual indicators, not proof of operation. A site described as "operating 351 days post-crackdown" on satellite evidence is a visual-indicator finding with a stated date and baseline, not a laboratory or inspection finding.
  • What enforcement does not show: the Department of Environment regularly shuts down or demolishes illegal smelters, including drives in Savar, Narsingdi, and Mymensingh, but there remains zero public record of permanent environmental soil remediation at any analysed coordinate.
  • Absence of a remediation record is not proof that no remediation ever occurred anywhere — it is a public-record finding at these coordinates, stated as such.

What to watch next

  • Whether any permanent remediation record appears at the analysed coordinates — closures and demolitions are not remediation.
  • Whether the Department of Environment publishes the full Environmental Clearance Certificate registry, and whether the 44 school-adjacent sites are inspected or served closure orders within the proposed 90 days.
  • Whether a national soil remediation programme is commissioned, prioritising the 18 schools within 500 metres of BD-4591.
  • Whether the master dataset v5 is restored to the repository, so per-site values can be independently re-run.
  • Whether any response is recorded to the March 2026 right of reply (72-hour window, published with or without response) — none is recorded in the appendix table.

Grades used on this page: Confirmed — two independent published sources agree, arithmetic re-checked. Supported — one strong published source, or two that agree with a stated caveat. Contested — published sources disagree, or a claim rests on a single source that is not independently verified; the range is shown, not a picked number. Untraced — no published source yet; never published as a finding, listed only as a gap.
