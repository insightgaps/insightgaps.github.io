Published investigation · BD-INV-003

The Lead Belt

Bangladesh's lead poisoning crisis mapped: 294 contaminated sites, 145 schools within 500 metres, and soil lead readings up to 1,702 times the international safety standard.

Published Mon May 25 2026 00:00:00 GMT+0000 (Coordinated Universal Time) · Revised Thu Sep 03 2026 00:00:00 GMT+0000 (Coordinated Universal Time)

In 60 seconds· Evidence in charts· The story, short· What we do not know· Method· Evidence· Right of reply· Corrections· What to watch next

In 60 seconds

  • Finding 1 (claim R-01, grade Confirmed): 294 contaminated sites are mapped across Bangladesh using Pure Earth and University of Dhaka assessment data, across 93 upazilas in all divisions.
  • Finding 2 (claim R-02, grade Confirmed): 145 school–site intersections place schools within 500 metres of contaminated sites, for the 44 formally assessed headline sites. The unique-school count for those sites is 121, and the unique-student estimate is about 33,275 — the 145 figure counts intersections, not unique schools, and the distinction is stated wherever the figure appears.
  • Finding 3 (claim R-03, grade Confirmed): The highest soil lead reading in the report reaches 680,872 ppm at Kathgora, Savar, about 1,702 times the 400 ppm international residential safety threshold.
  • Finding 4 (claim R-04, grade Confirmed): Zero contaminated sites have been permanently remediated or cleaned up, on the public record at the coordinates analysed. Enforcement closures and demolitions are not remediation, and the two must not be conflated.
  • Finding 5 (claim R-05, grade Supported): About 39,875 exposure instances are projected (145 intersections × 275 average enrolment), with a unique-school estimate of about 33,275 and an audited range of 24,650 to 50,750. This is a projection from an enrolment model, not a count of individually tested children.
  • Finding 6 (claim R-06, grade Confirmed): In UNICEF and Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics MICS 2025, 38.3% of children nationally and 65.2% in Dhaka Division exceed safe blood-lead limits, with Rajshahi lowest at 15.9% — a fourfold divisional difference in the live report's framing.
  • Finding 7 (claim R-07, grade Confirmed): Only six formal, licensed recyclers operate in Bangladesh, processing about 30% of national battery waste. More than 6,000 informal, unregistered workshops process about 70% without emission scrubbers.
  • Finding 8 (claim R-08, grade Confirmed): Across Bangladesh, 68 sites are designated critical zones, with 65 exceeding 100,000 ppm (250 times safe). The two numbers are different and must not be merged.

Satellite wording rule for this report, applied throughout: satellite imagery shows proximity and change. It does not measure soil or blood lead concentration. The wording is "satellite corroboration", never "satellite proof", and every satellite claim is paired with ground or laboratory data, a baseline, and a caveat.

The story, short

Scene 1: 59 metres from a school gate

Claim R-02 and R-03. In Rosulpur, Kamrangir Char, Dhaka, a battery recycling factory operates adjacent to primary school grounds. A child walks through the school gate each morning; the soil between the gate and the factory holds that lead, and lead does not biodegrade.

Key figures: 59 m, school gate to factory (BD-4591); soil lead 330,001 ppm — 825 times the US EPA residential threshold; 18 schools within 500 metres of this single furnace. Full featured-site table in "The evidence in charts" above.

Scene 2: A national burden, uneven by place

Claim R-06. Lead causes permanent cognitive deficit and organ damage. The divisional pattern in MICS 2025 is stark: location determines burden, with Dhaka at 65.2% and Rajshahi at 15.9%.

Key figures: Dhaka 65.2%, national 38.3%, Rajshahi 15.9% (MICS 2025, full table above). In a clinical cohort, not a population survey, 98% of 500 tested Dhaka toddlers aged 2–4 exceeded CDC reference standards (Sultana et al., Stanford / icddr,b, International Journal of Hygiene and Environmental Health 2026, peer-reviewed) — never quoted as a national or divisional rate. Separately, children living within 200 metres of the abandoned Kathgora smelter had a median blood lead of 21.3 µg/dL, 4.3 times safe, years after smelting ceased (icddr,b / Stanford, Environmental Research, PMID 33412099).

Scene 3: Formal versus informal — 6 against 6,000

Claim R-07. Only six formal, licensed recyclers operate in Bangladesh, processing about 30% of national battery waste. More than 6,000 informal, unregistered workshops process about 70% without emission scrubbers. In 2025, the Department of Environment shut down 28 facilities in Jatrabari.

Key distinction: A closure is an enforcement event; continued visual activity after a closure is a separate finding, and neither is remediation. Satellite verification in March 2026 confirmed visual indicators of continued activity at Site BD-4802, 351 days after the crackdown, while BD-4591 at Kamrangir Char was never targeted in that drive. Across Bangladesh, 68 sites are critical zones and 65 exceed 100,000 ppm (250 times safe) — different numbers, not to be merged.

Scene 4: 22 years of documentation, zero permanent cleanups

The live report's demands, stated as proposals, not findings, are: the Department of Environment publishes the full Environmental Clearance Certificate registry; all 44 school-adjacent sites are formally inspected and either issued certificates or served closure orders under Section 7 of the Bangladesh Environment Conservation Act 1995 within 90 days; and a national soil remediation programme is commissioned within one year, prioritising the 18 schools within 500 metres of BD-4591.

What we do not know

  • The published and reconstructed counts differ, and both are shown. Published: 44 sites with schools, 145 intersections, 121 unique schools for the headline sites.
  • Reconstructed counts: 51 sites, 166 intersections, 125 unique schools across all 294 sites in the provenance note; the methodology page also reports 132 unique schools and 166 intersections when counting schools newly mapped in OpenStreetMap.
  • The discrepancy is due to OpenStreetMap database drift between the original extraction window and the archived snapshot. It was not silently normalised: the published figures are retained as the publication of record.
  • The master dataset is not in the repository. The live investigation metadata references a Lead Belt master dataset v5 in Excel and CSV, with status "not-in-repository", and notes restoration is pending an owner decision.
  • Per-site values cannot be independently re-run from this draft alone. The spatial replication script (methods/analyze.py in the live repository) is referenced, not republished here, and that is a gap.
  • Exposure instances are not tested children. The 39,875 figure multiplies intersections by an average enrolment of 275 students per school (Annual Primary School Census 2024 / Directorate of Primary Education).
  • 39,875 must never be quoted as "39,875 children poisoned" or "children at extreme risk" — the 2026-09-03 correction reframed it exactly as a projection of exposure instances.
  • Historical blood-lead figures are not current figures. A 2000 Dhaka study (779 children, mean 15.0 µg/dL) belongs to the leaded-petrol era and must never be presented as current.
  • Current divisional figures in this report are MICS 2025, and clinical cohort figures are labelled by study, year, and sample.
  • Satellite activity indicators are visual indicators, not proof of operation. A site described as "operating 351 days post-crackdown" on satellite evidence is a visual-indicator finding with a stated date and baseline, not a laboratory or inspection finding.
  • What enforcement does not show: the Department of Environment regularly shuts down or demolishes illegal smelters, including drives in Savar, Narsingdi, and Mymensingh, but there remains zero public record of permanent environmental soil remediation at any analysed coordinate.
  • Absence of a remediation record is not proof that no remediation ever occurred anywhere — it is a public-record finding at these coordinates, stated as such.
Method and limitations

Sources: Pure Earth Contaminated Sites Index (2016–2019) and University of Dhaka assessment data for 294 sites; OpenStreetMap schools snapshot (9,846 nodes, March 2026 reconstructed snapshot, ODbL attribution); Directorate of Primary Education Annual Primary School Census 2024 for the 275-student average enrolment; UNICEF / BBS MICS 2025 for divisional blood-lead rates; icddr,b / Stanford clinical and peer-reviewed studies; Lancet Planetary Health 2023 for modelled deaths and economic toll; Department of Environment enforcement records and published reporting.

Method, in five stages as published: (1) GPS coordinates and maximum soil lead measurements for 294 assessed sites extracted from the Pure Earth database; (2) school locations queried from OpenStreetMap via Overpass Turbo centre centroid coordinates; (3) proximity calculated by the Haversine great-circle formula (Earth radius 6,371,000 metres, proximity if distance ≤ 500 metres), using the zero-dependency Python script analyze.py in the live repository; (4) student counts estimated at 275 students per school; (5) satellite imagery coordinate sweeps at Zoom-16 and Zoom-18 analysed for visual indicators of active processing.

Reconciliation, stated in full: published intersections (145) count each school once per site it is near, so 24 overlap instances produce 145 intersections from 121 unique schools for the 44 headline sites. Re-running against the archived snapshot gives 51 sites, 166 intersections, and 125 unique schools across all 294 sites in the provenance note, and the methodology page reports 132 unique schools and 166 intersections when newly mapped schools are included. These are different counting rules and different snapshots, not a single corrected number — and this report does not pick one silently.

Satellite method and limits: satellite imagery was analysed with an AI-assisted visual check (Gemini 2.0 Flash Lite in the live methodology) for visual indicators only. Satellite shows proximity and change; it does not measure soil lead, blood lead, or emissions. Every satellite claim needs sensor, date, resolution, cloud cover, baseline, and ground corroboration, and the live report's satellite pairs for BD-4591 and BD-4802 are described here as corroboration, not proof.

Public data only. No RTI, interview, or fieldwork is used in this migrated report. The live report states that an RTI request was filed with the Department of Environment and the Department of Inspection for Factories and Establishments in March 2026, requesting the full certificate list and closure orders 2016–2026, with a 30-day response deadline under the Right to Information Act 2009. Under this programme's public-data-only rule, that RTI trail is recorded as a published statement about the live report's method, not as a method step pursued here, and its outcome is not stated as known in this draft — that is a gap.
