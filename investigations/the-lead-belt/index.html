The Lead Belt

In Kamrangir Char, a primary school gate stands exactly 59 metres from an illegal used lead-acid battery furnace. Across Bangladesh, 294 contaminated sites have been mapped, peak soil lead levels reach 1,702 times residential safety limits, and 65.2% of children in Dhaka division suffer elevated blood lead. Yet across 22 years of official surveys, the public record of permanent soil cleanups stands at zero.

Step 1 · Proximity

59 Metres: School Gate to Battery Smelter

At site BD-4591 in Rosulpur, Kamrangir Char, a primary school gate stands exactly 59 metres from an active informal used lead-acid battery (ULAB) recycling furnace. Soil assays between the gate and furnace measure lead at 330,001 parts per million (ppm) — 825 times the 400 ppm residential safety threshold. Within a 500-metre screening perimeter around this single furnace, 18 educational institutions operate in high-density residential blocks.

Key Finding (Claims R-02, R-03 · Confirmed): 59m gate-to-factory distance; 330,001 ppm soil lead (825× residential limit); 18 schools within 500m of site BD-4591.

Step 2 · Peak Toxicity

1,702 Times Safe: Peak Concentration at Savar

Contamination reaches extreme levels in industrial suburban clusters. At Kathgora, Savar (site BD-4921), peak soil lead concentrations measure 680,872 ppm (rounded to 681,000 ppm) — 1,702 times the 400 ppm residential limit, and 568 times the 1,200 ppm industrial limit. At Kamrangir Char (BD-4591), soil lead reaches 330,001 ppm (825× limit). At Jatrabari (BD-4802), exceedance reaches 744× (though its underlying raw ppm figure remains unpublished in public release tables).

Key Finding (Claim R-03 · Confirmed): Peak reading of 680,872 ppm at Savar (BD-4921) is 1,702× safe limit. Jatrabari reaches 744× (ppm unprinted, flagged).

Step 3 · National Mapping

294 Sites Mapped: Reconciling the School Counts

Nationwide environmental mapping has identified 294 contaminated battery smelting sites across 93 upazilas in all divisions. Discrepancies exist across releases regarding affected schools: the published report record documents 44 investigated sites, 145 school buffer intersections, and 121 unique primary schools. An expanded rerun snapshot covering all 294 sites identified 51 sites within range, 166 intersections, and 125 unique schools; combined methodology pages list 132 unique schools.

Key Finding (Claim R-01 · Confirmed): 294 total contaminated sites. Published record of 44 sites / 145 intersections / 121 schools is reconciled with rerun counts (51/166/125) due to OpenStreetMap database drift.

Step 4 · Divisional Exposure

Childhood Blood Lead: 65.2% in Dhaka Division

Soil contamination correlates with widespread physiological poisoning. The national Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS 2025), conducted by the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics (BBS) and UNICEF, tested blood lead levels in children. In Dhaka division — the industrial hub of informal battery breaking — 65.2% of children have blood lead levels exceeding the 3.5 µg/dL clinical reference threshold. Nationally, 38.3% of children exceed safe limits, with Khulna at 48.0%, Sylhet at 46.7%, Chattogram at 42.1%, and Rajshahi at 15.9%.

Key Finding (Claim R-06 · Confirmed): 65.2% of children in Dhaka division have blood lead ≥3.5 µg/dL (MICS 2025). National baseline is 38.3%.

Step 5 · Market Failure

6 Formal Plants vs. 6,000+ Informal Smelters

The supply chain of vehicle and solar battery scrap is overwhelmingly informal. Bangladesh possesses only six licensed, formal battery recycling facilities equipped with automated breaker systems and chemical emission scrubbers, handling roughly 30% of national scrap. Over 6,000 informal, unlicensed workshops and backyard crucible smelters process the remaining 70%, operating without emission controls directly inside residential settlements.

Key Finding (Claim R-07 · Confirmed): 6 formal recyclers handle ~30% of battery waste; 6,000+ informal smelters process ~70% without pollution controls.

Step 6 · The Projection

39,875 Exposure Instances: A Projection, NOT Poisoned Children

Multiplying 145 school buffer intersections by the national average school enrolment of 275 pupils (Annual Primary School Census 2024) yields approximately 39,875 projected exposure instances. Based on 121 unique schools, the estimate is 33,275, with an audited projection range between 24,650 and 50,750 pupils. Rigorous data journalism establishes that this is a mathematical projection of potential student exposures, not a headcount of clinically tested or "poisoned" individuals.

Key Finding (Claim R-05 · Supported): 39,875 is a projection of student exposure instances (145 intersections × 275 pupils). Mandatory rule: NEVER quote as "children poisoned".

Step 7 · Critical Zones

68 Critical Zones, 65 Over 100,000 PPM

Environmental risk is classified into distinct severity tiers. Among the 294 mapped sites, 68 are designated critical contamination zones based on residential proximity and population density. Of those, 65 sites have measured soil lead concentrations exceeding 100,000 ppm — two hundred and fifty times the residential threshold. The three-site gap between 68 and 65 distinguishes institutional classification from chemical assay measurements.

Key Finding (Claim R-08 · Confirmed): 68 designated critical zones; 65 exceed 100,000 ppm (250× limit). The two numbers represent separate metrics.

Step 8 · 22-Year Timeline

22 Years of Official Surveys, Zero Cleanups

Government awareness spans over two decades without environmental remediation. In 2003, BBS documented 12,207 battery workshops nationwide. In 2019, Pure Earth and Dhaka University surveyed 270+ smelting sites. In 2021, severe poisoning was documented at an abandoned furnace in Savar. In February 2025, the Ministry of Environment signed its first formal remediation MoU. In April 2025, mobile courts shuttered 28 smelters in Jatrabari. Yet across this entire 22-year timeline, the official record of permanent soil decontaminations remains zero.

Key Finding (Claim R-04 · Confirmed): 22 years of official surveys (2003–2026), 0 permanent soil remediations on public record at analysed coordinates.

Step 9 · Closure vs. Cleanup

Enforcement Demolition is NOT Soil Remediation

Regulatory authorities frequently conflate administrative eviction with environmental remediation. When taskforces demolish smelting sheds or disconnect utility lines (such as the 28 closures in April 2025), the enforcement action halts active emissions, but operators simply move machinery to adjacent wards. Lead dust and toxic heavy metals remain chemically bonded in the soil. True remediation requires physical soil excavation, chemical encapsulation, and verified post-cleanup soil testing.

Key Finding (Claim R-04 · Confirmed): Administrative factory closure leaves toxic soil unchanged. Permanent remediation requires verified decontamination.

Step 10 · Remote Sensing Limits

Satellite Imagery is Corroboration, Never Proof

High-resolution satellite imagery (Sentinel-2 10m optical and commercial overhead sensors) provides temporal verification of facility expansion, shed construction, and chimney presence. However, satellite sensors cannot measure chemical parts-per-million in topsoil or detect lead particulate concentrations. Bureau policy strictly mandates that remote sensing constitutes structural corroboration, never independent chemical proof of soil contamination.

Key Finding (Method Standard): Satellite imagery confirms infrastructure and operational timelines. It cannot detect soil lead and must always pair with ground laboratory assays.

National Health & Economic Burden Model

According to epidemiological modelling published in The Lancet Planetary Health (2023), lead exposure in Bangladesh accounts for an estimated 138,054 cardiovascular deaths per year and an economic output loss of $28.63 billion annually (approx. 6% of GDP). This figure is audited as an epidemiological model of systemic cost, not a counted register of individual deaths.

Documented Gaps & Reconciliation Standards

  • Reconciliation of School Counts: Baseline published figures cite 44 sites, 145 intersections, and 121 unique schools. Rerun snapshots cite 51 sites, 166 intersections, and 125 unique schools; combined lists cite 132 unique schools. The variance reflects OpenStreetMap school tagging additions over time, not newly established factories.
  • Jatrabari Missing PPM Value: Site BD-4802 is cited at 744× exceedance, but its raw parts-per-million concentration is unprinted in public summary tables.
  • Remediation Scoping: The confirmed zero permanent cleanups finding applies strictly to analysed coordinates within published surveys; it documents the absence of public remediation records, not proof that unrecorded cleanups never occurred elsewhere.
