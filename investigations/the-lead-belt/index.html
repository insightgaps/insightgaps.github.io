The Lead Belt

In Kamrangir Char, a primary school gate stands exactly 59 metres from an illegal used lead-acid battery furnace. Across Bangladesh, 294 contaminated sites have been mapped, peak soil lead levels reach 1,702 times residential safety limits, and 65.2% of children in Dhaka division suffer elevated blood lead. Yet across 22 years of official surveys, the public record of permanent soil cleanups stands at zero.

The Missing Variable: Tanvir walks the perimeter of Nusrat's primary school in Kamrangir Char. VIGIL measures the perimeter: 59 paces from the classroom threshold to an open smelting crucible.
Decision point 1 — Decide first: why does an illegal lead furnace operate within 59 metres of primary classrooms without immediate closure?

Reveal — Claim R-02 · Confirmed. At Site BD-4591 in Kamrangir Char, an active furnace operated within 59 metres of a registered school gate. GIS screening confirms 145 school–site intersections within 500 metres across 44 headline sites, encompassing 121 unique schools and ~33,275 students.

1,702× TOXICITY Residential limit is 400 ppm. Soil at Kathgora tests at 680,872 ppm—seventeen hundred times safety ceiling.
The Missing Variable: Tanvir touches the dark furnace slag outside the school fence. VIGIL confirms the laboratory assay: 680,872 parts per million—pure industrial toxin embedded in residential soil.
Decision point 2 — Decide first: when soil lead reaches 1,702 times safe limits, is this pollution or an active disaster?

Reveal — Claim R-03 · Confirmed. The highest soil lead reading recorded reaches 680,872 ppm at Kathgora, Savar (Site BD-4921), about 1,702 times the 400 ppm residential safety threshold. Kamrangir Char reaches 330,001 ppm (825×). Sixty-five surveyed sites exceed 100,000 ppm.

The Veins: 65.2% of Dhaka Children Poisoned

65.2% ELEVATED BLL ≥3.5 µg/dL blood lead · 38.3% nationally (MICS 2025)
65.2% ELEVATED Two out of three children across Dhaka Division carry neurotoxic lead concentrations in their blood.
The Missing Variable: MUKTI pulls national health survey findings: the heavy metal is not confined to factory ground; it is circulating in the blood of two out of every three children in the capital.
Decision point 3 — Decide first: if two-thirds of children show elevated lead, why is paediatric screening absent from clinics?

Reveal — Claim R-06 · Confirmed. In the UNICEF and Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics MICS 2025 survey, 38.3% of children nationally and 65.2% in Dhaka Division exceed safe blood lead limits (≥3.5 µg/dL). Routine paediatric blood lead testing remains absent from public healthcare.

Screen 4 · National Contamination Audit

The Belt: 294 Sites Across 93 Upazilas

294 SITES 121 unique schools · 6,000 informal smelters handle 70% of scrap
294 SITES Over 6,000 informal smelters recycle 70% of national batteries, trapping 121 schools inside toxic perimeters.
The Missing Variable: VIGIL plots the nationwide spatial grid: Kamrangir Char is not an anomaly. Over 6,000 informal smelting operations form an industrial archipelago enveloping 33,000 primary students.
Decision point 4 — Decide first: how do 6,000 unlicensed backyard smelters process 70% of scrap battery waste openly?

Reveal — Claims R-01, R-05 & R-07 · Confirmed. 294 contaminated sites are mapped across 93 upazilas. Only six formal licensed recyclers operate in Bangladesh (processing ~30% of waste), while over 6,000 informal workshops melt the remaining 70%, driven by lower operating overheads.

Screen 5 · Institutional Policy Audit

The Record: 22 Years, Zero Remediations

0 CLEANUPS 22 years of official surveys (2003–2026) · Zero permanent remediations
ZERO CLEANUPS Twenty-two years of official inspection reports; zero permanent soil remediations ever completed on official record.
The Missing Variable: Tanvir searches the Department of Environment registry for soil decontamination certificates. MUKTI displays the circular cycle: enforcement raids smash brick chimneys, but leave poisoned soil for children to inhale.
Decision point 5 — Decide first: is smashing a furnace with sledgehammers enforcement, or does it scatter toxic dust further?

Reveal — Claim R-04 · Confirmed. Across twenty-two years of official monitoring (2003–2026), zero contaminated sites have been permanently remediated on the public record. Mobile court raids dismantle illegal kilns, but contaminated slag and soil remain untreated beside school boundaries.

The Lead Belt in Five Lines

  1. Unlicensed battery smelters operate 59 metres from primary school gates.
  2. Soil contamination reaches 680,872 ppm, over 1,700 times international safety limits.
  3. 65.2% of children across Dhaka Division carry elevated lead in their blood.
  4. 121 schools and 33,000 students sit within 500-metre contamination zones.
  5. In 22 years of documented surveys, zero toxic sites have ever been remediated.

Epidemiological & Macroeconomic Burden Model

According to epidemiological modelling published in The Lancet Planetary Health (2023), lead exposure in Bangladesh accounts for an estimated 138,054 cardiovascular deaths per year and an economic output loss of $28.63 billion annually (approx. 6% of GDP). This figure is audited as an epidemiological model of systemic cost, not a counted register of individual deaths.

Documented Gaps & Reconciliation Standards

  • School Count Reconciliation: Headline assessed baseline shows 44 sites, 145 intersections, and 121 unique schools (~33,275 students). Subsequent spatial runs incorporating updated OpenStreetMap school points yield 125 unique schools; combined catalogs list 132 unique schools. The variance reflects OSM dataset enrichment over time.
  • Remediation Verification: The finding of zero permanent cleanups applies strictly to documented records at surveyed geographic coordinates; it confirms the absence of public decontamination records.

Corrections Applied to This Investigation
