The Lead Belt — Evidence Appendix

This technical appendix records the methodology, audited spatial analysis, published official record, and corrections log for The Lead Belt investigation.

Methodology and Spatial Analysis

Primary Sources: Pure Earth Contaminated Sites Index (2016–2019) and University of Dhaka assessment data for 294 lead-contaminated sites across 93 upazilas in all eight divisions; OpenStreetMap schools snapshot (9,846 nodes, reconstructed March 2026 snapshot, ODbL attribution); Directorate of Primary Education (DPE) Annual Primary School Census 2024 for average institutional enrolment (275 students/school); UNICEF / Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics MICS 2025; icddr,b and Stanford University peer-reviewed clinical epidemiological studies (Kathgora Savar); Lancet Planetary Health (Larsen and Sánchez-Triana 2023); and Department of Environment enforcement gazettes.

Spatial Buffer & Exposure Modeling:

  • GPS coordinates and maximum XRF soil lead readings for 294 assessed sites were processed against school centroid coordinates.
  • Proximity was computed using the great-circle Haversine formula (Earth radius 6,371,000 metres; buffer threshold distance ≤ 500 metres).
  • Headline assessed sample: 44 sites intersect with educational facilities within 500 metres.
  • Because 24 schools overlap within multiple overlapping buffer zones, these 44 sites generate 145 school–site intersections across 121 unique schools.
  • Projected exposure instances: 145 intersections × 275 students = 39,875 exposure instances (audited uncertainty bounds: 24,650 to 50,750). Unique-school student exposure estimate: 121 unique schools × 275 = 33,275 children.
  • Peak soil lead concentrations:
    • Kathgora, Savar (BD-4921): 680,872 ppm (mg/kg) — 1,702× the 400 ppm international residential threshold.
    • Kamrangir Char, Dhaka (BD-4591): 330,001 ppm — 825× the 400 ppm threshold; located exactly 59 metres from the nearest school gate.
    • Permanent Remediation: Zero sites permanently remediated on the public record.
  • Satellite Imagery Standards: High-resolution optical imagery sweeps are evaluated as corroboration, never proof. Satellite sensors detect spatial proximity, roof material characteristics, and smoke deposits; they cannot measure soil lead ppm or biological lead burdens.

Published Official Record

Under the bureau's public-data protocol, subjective interviews and pre-publication right-of-reply exchanges have been replaced by documented published records from regulatory agencies and international health authorities:

Published Official Environmental and Public Health Records
Authority / Body Documented Published Record Official Finding / Regulatory Status
Department of Environment (DoE) DoE Enforcement Bulletins (April 2025 Mobile Court Operations) & Gazetted Closure Notices. Administrative closure of 28 informal smelting workshops in Jatrabari and Savar. Official registry confirms zero permanent chemical soil remediation executed at closed sites, and major urban sites (such as BD-4591 in Kamrangir Char) were omitted from the drive.
Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) Formal Memorandum of Understanding with Pure Earth & Development Partners (February 2025). First formal institutional acknowledgment in 22 years of widespread unmonitored lead acid battery recycling clusters; establishes national inventory mandate.
Directorate of Primary Education (DPE) Annual Primary School Census 2024. Official institutional baseline verifying average school enrolment of 275 pupils per government primary facility across urban and rural upazilas.
Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics (BBS) & UNICEF Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS) 2025 Lead Biomarker Module. Nationwide venous blood lead level (BLL) surveillance confirming systemic pediatric lead exceedance across all eight administrative divisions.
The Lancet Planetary Health Global Health Metrics Research (Larsen & Sánchez-Triana 2023, PMID 37708911). Epidemiological modeling attributing an estimated 138,054 annual cardiovascular deaths and a $28.63 billion annual economic loss (approx. 6% of GDP) in Bangladesh to lead exposure.
