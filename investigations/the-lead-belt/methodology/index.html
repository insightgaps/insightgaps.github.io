Methodology and Reproducibility: The Lead Belt

On this page: In short · Spatial analysis logic · What we do not know · OpenStreetMap snapshot · Reconciliation · Method and limitations · Evidence · Right of reply · Corrections · What to watch next

In short

  • Method: Haversine distance from every contaminated site to every OpenStreetMap school node; proximity if distance ≤ 500 metres.
  • Published counts: 145 intersections, 121 unique schools (44 headline sites), 39,875 exposure instances (145 × 275).
  • Re-run counts: 51 sites, 166 intersections, 125 unique schools across all 294 sites — different snapshot and counting rule, not a silent correction.
  • Satellite rule: satellite corroboration, never satellite proof; satellite shows proximity and change only.

Spatial analysis logic

The Lead Belt investigation maps the spatial proximity of educational institutions in Bangladesh to assessed lead-contaminated sites. The analysis uses a zero-dependency Python script, analyze.py, which calculates the distance between every contaminated site and every school node in the OpenStreetMap database.

Core formula, Haversine great-circle distance:

  • a = sin²(Δlat/2) + cos(lat1) × cos(lat2) × sin²(Δlon/2)
  • c = 2 × atan2(√a, √(1−a))
  • d = R × c, where R = 6,371,000 metres (mean radius of the Earth)
  • Proximity is established if d ≤ 500 metres, the screening threshold for residential lead soil exposure.

What we do not know

  • The master dataset v5 (Excel and CSV) referenced by the live investigation metadata is not in the website repository, with restoration pending an owner decision.
  • A full independent re-run from this draft alone is therefore not possible, and that gap is stated here rather than filled with a substitute dataset.

OpenStreetMap school snapshot and query

  • Dataset file: data/osm_schools.geojson in the live repository (archived snapshot, 9,846 nodes, ODbL attribution).
  • Snapshot date: March 2026 (reconstructed dataset).
  • Extraction query (Overpass Turbo): area Bangladesh, admin level 2, then nodes, ways, and relations tagged amenity=school, with out center for centroid conversion.
  • Deduplication: ways and relations converted to centroid points; nodes with duplicate coordinate pairs (rounded to 5 decimal places, about ±1.1 m) removed, to prevent duplicate mapping of the same physical school campus.

Reconciliation of school and student counts

Published numbers (proximity instances): 145 school–site intersections, 39,875 students (145 × 275).

Reconstructed numbers (unique counts): 121 unique schools for the 44 headline sites, 33,275 unique students for those sites (121 × 275). Across all 294 sites in the archived snapshot re-run: 51 sites, 166 intersections, 125 unique schools (provenance note, 2026-09-03); the methodology page also reports 132 unique schools and 166 intersections when newly mapped schools are included.

Discrepancy explanation, as published: (1) overlap counting versus unique counting — 24 overlap instances where a school falls within 500 m of more than one site, so the school and its students are counted once per site in the published instance total; (2) OpenStreetMap drift — re-running the live query in 2026 shows 7 new lead-contaminated sites with schools mapped within 500 m due to recent OpenStreetMap additions. The published report is locked to the historic March 2026 analysis, and the published figures are retained as the publication of record.

Method and limitations

Replication instructions, as published: run python methods/analyze.py --sites data/BD-INV-003_LeadBelt_MasterDataset_v5.csv --schools data/osm_schools.geojson --output data/osm_intersections.csv, with buffer default 500.0 metres and enrolment default 275 (Directorate of Primary Education census).

Satellite method: Zoom-16 and Zoom-18 imagery sweeps analysed for visual indicators of active processing. Satellite shows proximity and change only; it does not measure soil lead, blood lead, or emissions. Every satellite claim needs sensor, date, resolution, cloud cover, baseline, and ground corroboration, and the wording is "satellite corroboration", never "satellite proof".
