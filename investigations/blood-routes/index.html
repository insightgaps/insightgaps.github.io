Blood Routes

Bangladesh road fatalities kill at nearly six times the official count. The joma daily-lease contract forces drivers into high-speed competition, banned fleets reincarnate under new names on identical corridors, and four crucial government datasets remain locked from public scrutiny.

Screen 1 · Fatality Undercount Audit

The Gap: 5.8× Undercount

5.8× GAP 31,578 (WHO) vs. 5,480 (BRTA police FIR)
← 5.76× (5.8×) UNDERCOUNT WHO models 31,578 annual deaths; police record only 5,480. 82% vanish from official ledgers.
The Missing Variable: Tanvir searches for records of his sister Nusrat's holiday bus collision. VIGIL flags the systemic baseline: five out of every six road fatalities are erased before official statistics are compiled.
Decision point 1 — Decide first: would you report the police count, or the epidemiological model?

Reveal — Claim R-02 · Supported. BRTA records only police First Information Reports (FIRs), omitting victims who die in transit or reach out-of-court settlements. WHO applies the 30-day global standard (31,578 vs 5,480 = 5.76×, rounded to 5.8×). A report that cites only police tallies conceals 82% of transit fatalities.

Screen 2 · Travel Surge & Night Corridor

The Rush: 351 Deaths in 15 Days

351 DEATHS 15-day Eid-ul-Fitr rush · 41.56% night fatal window
351 FATALITIES 351 die in 15 travel days; night corridors turn fatal as freight races empty streets.
The Missing Variable: Nusrat's coach departed Gabtoli after dark. MUKTI asks: Why does fatal risk spike when streets are empty? Unregulated heavy trucks take over arterial highways between 10pm and 6am.
Decision point 2 — Decide first: is night speeding a driver defect, or a dispatch mandate?

Reveal — Claims R-01 & R-07 · Supported & Confirmed. During Eid-ul-Fitr 2026, 351 deaths occurred across 15 days (23.4/day, reported). In Dhaka, 41.56% of fatal crashes occur between 10pm and 6am, and pedestrians make up 47% of victims. City entry bans push heavy trucks into narrow nocturnal speed windows.

Screen 3 · Commercial Incentive Audit

The Contract: Built to Force Racing

BDT 4,000–6,000 daily lease fee · Tk 8,000 on Eid · Driver nets BDT 300–500
FIXED DAILY DEBT Drivers start dawn Tk 5,000 in debt. To survive, they must race each other for fares.
The Missing Variable: Tanvir examines the bus driver's daily collection sheet. VIGIL confirms: the contract mathematically mandates competitive overtaking before the driver earns a single taka.
Decision point 3 — Decide first: who is legally liable for the race—the driver or the lessor?

Reveal — Claim R-03 · Confirmed. The joma system pays drivers zero base salary. The bus owner takes a guaranteed BDT 4,000–6,000 fixed daily rent (rising to Tk 8,000+ on Eid). The driver bears fuel and extortions, netting only BDT 300–500. Speed is an economic imperative built into the lease.

Screen 4 · Regulatory Capture Audit

The Phoenix Return: Banned Fleets Reborn

SAME CORRIDOR Suprobhat banned 2019 · Same chassis return under new names
ROUTE CONTINUITY A deadly bus line is revoked; weeks later identical chassis roll under fresh company paint.
The Missing Variable: Tanvir searches for the transport company that struck Nusrat's vehicle. MUKTI shows the loophole: Suprobhat was banned by court order, but its exact buses re-emerged under Victor Classic decals.
Decision point 4 — Decide first: is cancelling a fleet name regulation, or regulatory theater?

Reveal — Claim R-04 · Probable route continuity / Alleged ownership. Regulatory revocations target brand names rather than physical chassis numbers. Vehicles and drivers remain on the Sadarghat–Gazipur arterial route under new registrations, circumventing safety sanctions.

Screen 5 · Accountability Architecture Audit

The Four Voids: Data Held, Not Published

4 HIDDEN REGISTRIES Chassis crashes · Trauma linkage · Operator FIRs · Beneficial owners
FOUR VOIDS State holds chassis logs and owner registries behind closed doors, shielding commercial transport syndicates.
The Missing Variable: VIGIL hits the state administrative firewall. Tanvir asks why chassis collision logs are sealed. MUKTI: When beneficial owners are invisible, liability can never climb past the wage driver.
Decision point 5 — Decide first: why do police FIRs record the driver, but never the route-permit owner?

Reveal — Claim R-05 · Confirmed. The government maintains four operational datasets that remain unpublished: (1) chassis crash logs, (2) hospital trauma-to-crash records, (3) operator-named FIRs, and (4) route permit beneficial ownership. Without these, commercial fleet owners face zero systemic liability.

Blood Routes in Five Lines

  1. Official records log 5,480 road deaths; global public health models establish 31,578.
  2. The daily joma lease forces drivers to race for survival before clearing guaranteed debt.
  3. Revoked bus fleets reincarnate on identical corridors with identical chassis.
  4. Four unpublished registries insulate commercial fleet owners from legal culpability.
  5. The system does not fail by accident; it operates exactly as structured.
