Published investigation · BD-INV-001

Blood Routes

351 people died in 15 days during the 2026 Eid-ul-Fitr rush. Bangladesh's roads kill at six times the official count. The joma incentive system, political shields, and four critical data voids make it structural — not accidental.

Published Tue May 26 2026 00:00:00 GMT+0000 (Coordinated Universal Time) · Revised Thu Sep 03 2026 00:00:00 GMT+0000 (Coordinated Universal Time)

In 60 seconds· Evidence in charts· The story, short· What we do not know· Method· Evidence· Right of reply· Corrections· What to watch next

In 60 seconds

  • Finding 1 (claim R-01, grade Supported): During the 15-day Eid-ul-Fitr 2026 travel period, 351 people were killed, averaging 23 deaths per day — 23.4 by the bureau's own division, 351 ÷ 15.
  • Finding 2 (claim R-02, grade Supported): The WHO-modelled road death estimate, about 31,578 per year, is 5.8 times the BRTA official count of 5,480. The gap is maintained because hospital deaths are not linked to crash records and crashes settled before police are never counted.
  • Finding 3 (claim R-03, grade Confirmed): The joma daily-lease system forces bus drivers to race for passengers: drivers pay a fixed daily fee before earning, so racing is the rational response to the contract, not only individual recklessness. Attributed to Transparency International Bangladesh (TIB) 2024.
  • Finding 4 (claim R-04, grade Contested): Banned fleets re-emerge under new operator names on the same routes — the Phoenix pattern. Route continuity is probable; ownership continuity is alleged, not confirmed by a corporate registry, and no operator is found guilty unless a specific conviction is cited.
  • Finding 5 (claim R-05, grade Confirmed): Four datasets the government holds are not published: a BRTA bus crash registry by chassis, hospital trauma records linked to crash cause, police FIRs linked to operator name, and a route-permit beneficial-owner registry. Each absence is a documented accountability gap, not a technical limitation.
  • Finding 6 (claim R-06, grade Confirmed): The deduplicated dataset — 47,680 raw rows reduced to 2,331 unique accidents — shows heavy clustering at highway entry and exit corridors and roundabouts.
  • Finding 7 (claim R-07, grade Confirmed): 41.56% of fatal Dhaka crashes occur between 10pm and 6am, and pedestrians account for 47% of Dhaka road victims (Road Safety Foundation).

The story, short

Scene 1: Two numbers, one counting choice

Claim R-02. The official tally counts only incidents logged in police First Information Reports. Victims who die on the way to hospital, or whose families settle out of court, are not in it. Neither number is "the truth" on its own: the point is the definition gap, and that the gap is knowable and publishable.

Key figure: WHO modelled 31,578 per year vs BRTA official 5,480 — 5.8×.

Scene 2: Why buses race — the joma contract

Claim R-03. Under the joma system, private bus owners lease the bus to a driver-helper team for a fixed daily fee before fuel, helper wages, and gate fees. The driver must cover those costs before earning personal income. Racing for passengers first is what that contract rewards.

Key figures: Daily fee BDT 4,000 to BDT 6,000 (TIB), rising to Tk 8,000 or more during Eid; driver net income BDT 300 to BDT 500 per day. Figures are TIB estimates, not independently replicated by the bureau.

Scene 3: The Phoenix pattern

Claim R-04. After a fatal 2019 crash, the High Court and BRTA banned Suprobhat Paribahan from the Sadarghat–Gazipur corridor. The live report documents the same physical chassis continuing under shell cooperative names, including Victor Classic and Akash Paribahan, on identical routes. No beneficial ownership is asserted for any entity, and no operator is described as guilty unless a specific conviction is cited.

Key distinction: Route continuity is probable; ownership continuity is alleged, based on family and victim testimony, not a corporate registry.

Scene 4: Where crashes cluster, and when

Claims R-06 and R-07. The deduplicated dataset (47,680 raw rows reduced to 2,331 unique accidents, because 95.11% of raw rows were artificial padding created by cross-joining single accidents across permuted metadata values) shows heavy clustering at highway entry and exit corridors and roundabouts, and a night enforcement void.

Key figures: 41.56% of fatal Dhaka crashes between 10pm and 6am; pedestrians 47% of Dhaka road victims (Road Safety Foundation). The live report's scene heading says "2,332 fatal collisions" while its audit text says 2,331 unique incidents. This report uses 2,331, the audited deduplicated count, and flags the one-record difference as an unresolved inconsistency in the live text.

Scene 5: The four voids

Claim R-05. VOID-01: BRTA bus crash registry by vehicle chassis — would confirm or deny Phoenix ownership chains and enable operator-level death rates; BRTA holds it, classified, not published. VOID-02: hospital trauma records linked to crash cause — the only ground truth for the 5.8× undercount; paper records, undigitised, unconnected. VOID-03: police FIRs linked to operator name — FIRs record registration numbers, not operator brands; one recording-policy change would make operator accountability possible at scale. VOID-04: route-permit beneficial-owner registry — many permits are held by front companies or politically connected individuals; publication would expose political ownership of the bus sector.

The live report's reforms, stated as proposals, not findings, are: abolish the joma/waybill system in favour of monthly salary contracts; publish a chassis-linked public vehicle registry; enforce owner accountability already provided for under the Road Transport Act 2018; and create an independent accident investigation body separate from BRTA and police.

What we do not know

  • Eid-2026 headline period is outside the cleaned dataset's range. The cleaned dataset covers 2,331 unique accidents in 2022–2024.
  • The 351 Eid-2026 deaths are reported, not re-computed. They come from Road Safety Foundation and Bangladesh Jatri Kalyan Samity reporting, and the underlying reports are not yet archived in the bureau's evidence repository.
  • Operator attribution is not a registry fact. Police and government databases systematically omit operator names.
  • Live-report operator names are assigned by regional routing rules in the internal analysis, and its map provenance note states operator names are not assigned in the public download because the crash log does not carry a reliable operator field. Do not read an operator name on a map point as a registry finding.
  • The 10-year map is a distribution, not a decade of observed points. The raw Figshare dataset's Year column was decoupled padding; true incident dates are 2022–2024.
  • That distribution spreads the 2,331 deduplicated accidents proportionally to annual Jatri Kalyan Samity fatalities. That construction must be labelled as such wherever the map is shown.
  • Whether publishing the four held datasets would confirm or deny any Phoenix ownership chain — unknown.
  • What share of crashes are settled before police — unknown.
  • How many victims die after hospital admission — unknown, because hospital deaths are not linked to crash records.
Method and limitations

Sources: WHO modelled estimates, BRTA official counts, Road Safety Foundation and Bangladesh Jatri Kalyan Samity annual and Eid monitoring reports, Transparency International Bangladesh 2024 transport survey, World Bank 2022 vehicle-rate comparison (102 deaths per 10,000 registered vehicles, 19.3 per 100,000 population, WHO 2023), and the Figshare academic dataset "Bangladesh Road Traffic Accident Dataset (2007–2024): Multi-Source Integration" (DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.30631292.v1), 47,680 rows × 31 columns.

Cleaning steps: rows grouped by nine identifying fields (location, parsed date, time, latitude, longitude, driver age, accident narrative, vehicle info, victim category); fatality and injury counts aggregated by maximum per incident; result 2,331 unique accidents (4.89% of raw rows). The raw Year and Month columns were discarded as mismatched padding; true dates were parsed from date strings, giving 2022–2024 (2022: 334, 2023: 4, 2024: 1,993 unique crashes in the audit text — note the 2023 count of 4 is itself a data-quality warning, not a real annual crash count). 143 rows contained the string "IMG 2467" instead of a valid date, indicating camera metadata mistakenly integrated into the date column.

Definitions and sensitivity: BRTA counts police-FIR spot-recorded deaths; WHO uses a modelled 30-day definition. Changing the definition changes the total by a factor of about 5.8, which is the finding — not a rounding difference. Dhaka map points are exact source coordinates; non-Dhaka points are visual corridor placements, not street-level claims, per the live report's map provenance note.

Public data only. No RTI, interview, or fieldwork is used in this migrated report. The live report mentions an RTI application as possible for the beneficial-owner registry; this programme does not pursue RTI, so that route is stated as a gap, not a method step here.

What to watch next

  • Whether the four held datasets are published — the BRTA chassis registry, linked hospital trauma records, operator-linked FIRs, and the beneficial-owner registry. Publication would test the Phoenix ownership chain and the 5.8× undercount.
  • Whether the Eid-2026 underlying reports are archived in the bureau's evidence repository, so the 351 figure can be re-computed rather than reported.
  • Whether a fresh, logged reply attempt is made before any new allegation beyond what the live report already published — BRTA and the Phoenix operators have no recorded reply in the table above.
  • Whether the flagged inconsistencies are resolved — the 2,332 vs 2,331 scene-heading difference, and the manifest revised date of 2026-06-08 vs the 2026-09-03 corrections.
