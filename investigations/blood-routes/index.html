Blood Routes in Five Lines

  1. Official records log 5,480 road deaths; global public health models establish 31,578.
  2. The daily joma lease forces drivers to race for survival before clearing guaranteed debt.
  3. Revoked bus fleets reincarnate on identical corridors with identical chassis.
  4. Four unpublished registries insulate commercial fleet owners from legal culpability.
  5. The system does not fail by accident; it operates exactly as structured.

The Verdict Handed to the Reader

Is Dhaka's road transit crisis a failure of individual driver discipline, or an institutional contract design engineered for impunity?

Decide-First Inquiries & Empirical Findings

1. Police vs Modelled Count: BRTA records only police First Information Reports (FIRs), omitting in-transit deaths and informal settlements. WHO applies the 30-day global standard (31,578 vs 5,480 = 5.76×, rounded to 5.8×). Citing only police tallies conceals 82% of fatalities.

2. Night Speeding Mechanics: During Eid-ul-Fitr 2026, 351 deaths occurred across 15 days (23.4/day, reported). In Dhaka, 41.56% of fatal crashes occur between 10pm and 6am, and pedestrians make up 47% of victims. City entry bans push heavy trucks into narrow nocturnal speed windows.

3. Contractual Liability: The joma system pays drivers zero base salary. The bus owner takes guaranteed rent of BDT 4,000–6,000/day (Tk 8,000+ Eid), leaving drivers BDT 300–500 net. Speed is an economic imperative built into the lease.

4. Fleet Rebranding: Revocations target brand names rather than physical chassis numbers. Vehicles and drivers remain on the Sadarghat–Gazipur route under new registrations.

5. Missing Registries: The state holds chassis crash logs, hospital trauma linkages, operator-named FIRs, and route permit beneficial ownership records behind closed doors.
