Published investigation · BD-INV-001

Blood Routes

351 people died in 15 days during the 2026 Eid-ul-Fitr rush. Bangladesh's roads kill at six times the official count. The joma incentive system, political shields, and four critical data voids make it structural — not accidental.

The story in visuals

Step 1 · Claim R-02 · Supported

Two numbers, one counting choice

The official tally counts only incidents logged in police First Information Reports. Victims who die on the way to hospital, or whose families settle out of court, are not in it.

The WHO model uses a 30-day, modelled definition. The gap is knowable and publishable — that is the finding, not a rounding difference.

Step 2 · Claim R-06 · Confirmed

Crashes cluster at corridors and roundabouts

The deduplicated dataset shows heavy clustering at highway entry and exit corridors and roundabouts. These are the places where enforcement, design, and speed meet.

See the next step for how the raw data was cleaned before any hotspot could be trusted.

Step 3 · Claim R-06 · Confirmed

47,680 rows became 2,331 accidents

Rows were grouped by nine identifying fields and aggregated by maximum per incident. About 95% of raw rows were artificial padding, created by cross-joining single accidents across permuted metadata values.

The live scene heading says 2,332; this report uses 2,331, the audited count, and flags the one-record difference as unresolved.

Step 4 · Claim R-07 · Confirmed

The night is the enforcement void

41.56% of fatal Dhaka crashes occur between 10pm and 6am. Pedestrians account for 47% of Dhaka road victims.

Correction C-001 matters here: the original 97.13% night share came from a dataset later found to contain about 95% synthetic padding, and was retracted on 2026-06-03.

Step 5 · Claim R-01 · Supported

351 deaths in 15 days

During the 15-day Eid-ul-Fitr 2026 travel period, 351 people were killed — about 23 per day. That headline period sits outside the cleaned dataset's 2022–2024 range, so it is reported, not re-computed from the bureau's dataset.

Correction C-002: the Eid-ul-Adha description was corrected from a "29% surge" to a 19% rise, 312 vs 262.

Step 6 · Claim R-03 · Confirmed

Why buses race: the joma contract

Private owners lease the bus to a driver-helper team for a fixed daily fee, before fuel, helper wages, and gate fees. The driver must cover those costs before earning personal income.

Racing for passengers first is what that contract rewards. These figures are TIB estimates, not independently replicated by the bureau.

Step 7 · Claim R-04 · Contested

The Phoenix pattern

After a fatal 2019 crash, the High Court and BRTA banned Suprobhat Paribahan from the Sadarghat–Gazipur corridor. The live report documents the same physical chassis continuing under shell cooperative names, including Victor Classic and Akash Paribahan, on identical routes.

No beneficial ownership is asserted, and no operator is described as guilty unless a specific conviction is cited.

VOID-01 · Chassis registry

BRTA bus crash registry by vehicle chassis — would confirm or deny Phoenix ownership chains. Held, classified, not published.

VOID-02 · Hospital records

Hospital trauma records linked to crash cause — the only ground truth for the 5.8× undercount. Paper, undigitised, unconnected.

VOID-03 · Operator-linked FIRs

Police FIRs linked to operator name. FIRs record registration numbers, not operator brands.

VOID-04 · Beneficial owners

Route-permit beneficial-owner registry. Many permits are held by front companies or politically connected individuals.

Step 8 · Claim R-05 · Confirmed

Four datasets the government holds, and does not publish

Each absence is a documented accountability gap, not a technical limitation. Publishing them would test the Phoenix chain and the 5.8× undercount directly.

Reforms proposed in the live report, not findings: abolish joma in favour of monthly salary contracts; publish a chassis-linked registry; enforce owner accountability under the Road Transport Act 2018; create an independent accident investigation body.

What we do not know

  • Eid-2026 headline period is outside the cleaned dataset's range. The cleaned dataset covers 2,331 unique accidents in 2022–2024.
  • The 351 Eid-2026 deaths are reported, not re-computed. They come from Road Safety Foundation and Bangladesh Jatri Kalyan Samity reporting, and the underlying reports are not yet archived in the bureau's evidence repository.
  • Operator attribution is not a registry fact. Police and government databases systematically omit operator names.
  • Live-report operator names are assigned by regional routing rules in the internal analysis, and its map provenance note states operator names are not assigned in the public download because the crash log does not carry a reliable operator field.
  • The 10-year map is a distribution, not a decade of observed points. The raw Figshare dataset's Year column was decoupled padding; true incident dates are 2022–2024.
  • That distribution spreads the 2,331 deduplicated accidents proportionally to annual Jatri Kalyan Samity fatalities. That construction must be labelled as such wherever the map is shown.
  • Whether publishing the four held datasets would confirm or deny any Phoenix ownership chain — unknown.
  • What share of crashes are settled before police — unknown.
  • How many victims die after hospital admission — unknown, because hospital deaths are not linked to crash records.

What to watch next

  • Whether the four held datasets are published — the BRTA chassis registry, linked hospital trauma records, operator-linked FIRs, and the beneficial-owner registry.
  • Whether the Eid-2026 underlying reports are archived in the bureau's evidence repository, so the 351 figure can be re-computed rather than reported.
  • Whether a fresh, logged reply attempt is made before any new allegation beyond what the live report already published — BRTA and the Phoenix operators have no recorded reply in the appendix table.
  • Whether the flagged inconsistencies are resolved — the 2,332 vs 2,331 scene-heading difference, and the manifest revised date of 2026-06-08 vs the 2026-09-03 corrections.
