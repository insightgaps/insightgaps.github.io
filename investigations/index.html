BD-INV-002 1 March 2026

Blood Routes

351 people died in 15 days during the 2026 Eid-ul-Fitr rush. Bangladesh's roads kill at six times the official count. The joma incentive system, political shields, and four critical data voids make it structural — not accidental.

methodology
  1. During the 15-day Eid-ul-Fitr 2026 travel period, 351 people were killed — averaging 23 deaths per day during the holiday rush.
  2. The WHO-modelled road death estimate (~31,578/year) is 5.8× higher than the BRTA official count (5,480) — a gap maintained by deliberate policy, not technical incapacity.
  3. The joma (daily deposit) system forces bus drivers to race for passengers under a guaranteed-income model that protects owners and transfers all lethal risk to drivers and pedestrians, 47% of Dhaka victims.

  • available Division boundaries (GeoJSON)

The Lead Belt

Bangladesh's lead poisoning crisis mapped: 294 contaminated sites, 145 schools within 500 metres, and soil lead readings up to 1,702 times the international safety standard.

methodology
  1. 294 contaminated sites are mapped across Bangladesh using Pure Earth / University of Dhaka assessment data.
  2. 145 schools are identified within 500 metres of contaminated sites, with the closest school in the report sitting 59 metres from a lead-acid battery recycling site.
  3. The highest soil lead reading in the report reaches 680,872 ppm, about 1,702 times the 400 ppm international residential safety threshold.

The Impunity Machine

A visual data investigation into Bangladesh's rape justice crisis and the institutional machinery that turns reported violence into near-zero punishment.

detailed methodology tracker
  1. Only 310 convictions were recorded from 66,711 women who came through One-Stop Crisis Centres over 23 years, a 0.46% conviction rate.
  2. Bangladesh pairs maximum legal severity with near-zero certainty of punishment: death penalty expansion did not solve reporting attrition, backlog, evidence loss, or witness risk.
  3. Critical justice infrastructure remains thin: 14 OCCs, two national DNA machines, major unprocessed sample backlogs, and no complete national registry for key rape-case outcomes.
