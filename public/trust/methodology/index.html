Methodology

Insight Gaps Bureau publishes only what can be traced to a primary source, verified against the evidence tier appropriate to that investigation type, and defended under scrutiny. This page documents the standards that govern every piece of published work.

What counts as verified

A claim is considered verified when it can be traced to a primary source — an original document, a first-party dataset, a government record, or a direct on-record account — and when that source has been assessed for reliability against the standards of the investigation's tier.

Secondary reporting, aggregated statistics, and uncorroborated assertions are not treated as verified. They may appear as context but are labelled as such. No central finding is built on a secondary source alone.

Verification tiers

Verification standards are not universal. Different investigation types carry different evidentiary obligations. Every investigation is assigned one of three tiers before reporting begins, and that tier governs what is required before publication.

Tier 1

Document-Heavy Investigation

Applies to: regulatory failures, environmental records, public health data, institutional accountability.

  • The central claim requires a traceable primary document.
  • All statistics require their original source, not secondary reporting.
  • Government records are preferred over NGO summaries unless the NGO holds primary data.
  • Geographic claims require coordinate verification.
Tier 2

Data-Driven Investigation

Applies to: quantitative analysis, pattern detection, comparative datasets.

  • Analysis must be reproducible from raw data using documented scripts.
  • No manual edits to raw data files — all transformations are performed via script only.
  • Outliers must be explained, not removed without documentation.
  • A methodology note is mandatory before publication.
Tier 3

Source-Driven Investigation

Applies to: testimony, interviews, field observation.

  • A minimum of two independent sources is required for any central claim.
  • Source identity is protected in all internal notes — role descriptors, not names.
  • On-record versus off-record status is documented for every source before drafting begins.
  • No claim from a single anonymous source is published without corroborating documentary evidence.

Data handling rules

Raw data files are never altered. All analytical transformations — cleaning, aggregation, derivation — are performed via documented scripts. The transformation chain from raw file to published figure is preserved and available for review.

  • Raw files are stored in a read-only state in the investigation archive.
  • Transformation scripts are version-controlled alongside the investigation.
  • Every published statistic traces to a specific row or field in the source data.
  • Outliers are flagged, not silently removed.
  • Derived figures — totals, averages, percentages — are calculated from primary data, not secondary sources.

Source protection

Sources who provide information under conditions of confidentiality are never identified in published work or internal files. Internal notes use role descriptors only — for example, "Government Inspector, Gazipur" — not names or contact details. Sensitive source information is stored outside the investigation repository on encrypted local storage.

If a source requests removal after contributing information, that request is assessed and decided by the editor — not automated or delegated to any tool.

Corrections

Errors are corrected promptly and logged permanently on the corrections page. Correction entries are never deleted. The standard is: corrections go up faster than the original publication went out.

AI use in reporting

AI tools assist at specific stages of research and production. No AI tool owns editorial decisions, final verification, or the decision to publish.

Full disclosure of which tools are used, in what roles, and what they are explicitly prohibited from owning.
