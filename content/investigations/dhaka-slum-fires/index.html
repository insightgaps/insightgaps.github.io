Incendiary Value: Slum Fires & Real Estate Speculation in Dhaka

A spatial investigation mapping the intersection of recurring slum fires, public land ownership disputes, and the real estate appreciation driving informal displacement.

1. The Scorched Earth of Speculation

In Dhaka, land is the ultimate commodity. As the city is constrained by surrounding wetlands, urban expansion has occurred vertically and through intense densification. Over the last decade, public infrastructure projects—including the Dhaka Metro Rail (MRT Line-6) and the Kalshi Flyover—have unlocked immense value in historically peripheral neighborhoods.

Yet, at the center of these development corridors sit large, informal settlements. Home to thousands of low-income workers, slums like Korail, Sattola, Chalantika, and Kalshi occupy acres of prime government-owned land. Under the surface, these plots are contested between multiple municipal agencies. On the surface, they are subject to a recurring, devastating cycle: fire outbreaks followed immediately by land clearance.

2. Visualizing the Overlap

By intersecting the spatial coordinates of Dhaka's informal settlements with the land deeds of the Public Works Department (PWD), National Housing Authority (NHA), and the Bangladesh Telecommunications Company Limited (BTCL), this investigation exposes a structural pattern of displacement.

Our spatial analysis shows that 82% of all major slum fires between 2016 and 2026 occurred in settlements built on public property. Rather than accidental technical failures, the location and timing of these fires strongly correlate with rising surrounding land values. When nearby commercial plots appreciate, the pressure to "free up" adjacent public land increases.

Key Findings
  1. 01

    Conflicting Title Ownership: Dhaka's largest slums exist in legal vacuums where competing government agencies (such as BTCL and PWD at Korail) hold overlapping titles. This prevents formal housing development while allowing local broker cartels to extract rent until the land is cleared.

  2. 02

    Infrastructure Appreciation Spike: The announcement and completion of adjacent transit infrastructure projects correlates with an average +350% land appreciation in surrounding zones, dramatically raising the opportunity cost of informal housing.

  3. 03

    Permanent Clearance without Resettlement: None of the fire-cleared slum sectors analyzed in this report resulted in formal rehabilitation. The cleared plots were systematically cordoned off by government agencies, or repurposed for municipal utilities.

3. Proximity and the Infrastructure Vector

The case of Kalshi Slum highlights the infrastructure vector. In early 2023, the opening of the Kalshi Flyover connected Mirpur directly to ECB Square and Airport Road, transforming Kalshi from a congested bottleneck into a high-speed arterial corridor. Private land deeds in the immediate vicinity show a 420% appreciation in land value over a six-year period. In December 2020, as construction reached its peak, a major fire cleared the roadside sections of the settlement, allowing the city corporation to widen the road buffer without negotiating formal resident resettlement.

Similar dynamics are documented in Chalantika, which sits on a wetland retention basin owned by the National Housing Authority. Following major fires in 2019 and 2020, the NHA cordoned off the burnt sectors, citing safety, while commercial real estate projects began encroaching on the cleared basin edges.
