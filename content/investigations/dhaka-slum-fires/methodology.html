Incendiary Value: Spatial & Land Appreciation Methodology

Released: May 28, 2026 — Sourced from Sub-Registrar Deeds, FSCD Records, and OSM Boundary Files

1. Introduction & Spatial Analysis Logic

The "Incendiary Value" investigation traces the spatial and economic correlations between fire incidents in Dhaka's informal settlements (slums) and surrounding real estate price indexes. The core thesis explores whether recurring fires act as an unofficial mechanism for land clearing in areas that experience high infrastructure-driven land value appreciation.

Our analysis utilizes spatial overlay calculations to correlate the georeferenced boundaries of four major settlements—Korail, Chalantika, Sattola, and Kalshi—with municipal development zones and proximity buffer zones of key transit hubs (MRT Line-6 stations and major flyovers).

Core Mathematical Formula (Haversine Distance to Infrastructure Hubs)

To compute the great-circle distance between a slum polygon centroid $(lat_1, lon_1)$ and the nearest transit project junction $(lat_2, lon_2)$, we utilize the standard Haversine formula:

a = sin²(Δlat/2) + cos(lat1) * cos(lat2) * sin²(Δlon/2)
c = 2 * atan2(√a, √(1-a))
d = R * c

Where:

  • R = 6,371,000 meters (Earth's mean radius).
  • d is the straight-line buffer distance. Intersections are evaluated for buffers at 500m and 1000m thresholds.

2. Land Value Appreciation Index Model

Land transaction valuations in Dhaka are dual-indexed to capture the difference between official and market realities:

  1. Official Deed Records (Sub-Registrar Offices): Data was harvested from the deed databases of the Sub-Registrar Offices in Gulshan, Tejgaon, Mirpur, and Pallabi. These record the declared transaction value ($V_{\text{official}}$).
  2. Broker-Developer Surveys: Formal market surveys conducted in adjacent residential blocks (such as Niketon for Sattola, Banani/Gulshan-1 for Korail, Mirpur DOHS for Kalshi) establish the actual average market price per katha ($V_{\text{market}}$).

The Land Value Appreciation Index represents the percentage change in the median market value per decimal of land between 2016 ($t_0$) and 2026 ($t_1$):

Appreciation (\%) = [ ( V_market(t1) - V_market(t0) ) / V_market(t0) ] * 100

3. Fire Frequency and Spatial Clustering

Fire records were obtained from the Bangladesh Fire Service & Civil Defence (FSCD) incident logs for the period 2016–2026. Data extraction was constrained using the following criteria:

  • Geocoding validation: Cross-referencing raw text addresses in FSCD reports (e.g., "Korail Jheelpar", "Kalshi Bosti") with coordinate boundaries extracted from the March 2026 OpenStreetMap shapefile.
  • Severity Threshold: Only fires recorded as "Medium" or "Severe" (requiring more than 4 fire service vehicles or taking over 2 hours to extinguish) were counted in the displacement metrics.

4. Python Implementation (Spatial Intersect Mock)

The analysis was performed using standard spatial packages. Below is the replication logic used to count spatial intersection instances: 

import geopandas as gpd
from shapely.geometry import Point

def analyze_overlay(slum_shape_path, infra_points_path, buffer_meters=500):
    # Load files and project to local metric UTM zone (WGS 84 / UTM zone 46N)
    slums = gpd.read_file(slum_shape_path).to_crs(epsg=32646)
    infra = gpd.read_file(infra_points_path).to_crs(epsg=32646)
    
    # Create metric buffer around infrastructure
    infra['geometry'] = infra.geometry.buffer(buffer_meters)
    
    # Intersect layers to find overlaps
    overlaps = gpd.overlay(slums, infra, how='intersection')
    
    print(f"Detected {len(overlaps)} spatial overlay intersections.")
    return overlaps
