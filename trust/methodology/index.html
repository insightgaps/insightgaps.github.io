Methodology

  1. Evidence before narrative. A story is built from a claim register, not from a headline.
  2. One claim, one grade. Every claim is graded Confirmed, Supported, Contested, or Untraced. Untraced claims are not published as findings.
  3. Grade mapping for older reports. The live reports used the tiers CONFIRMED, PROBABLE, ALLEGED, and CONFIRMED ABSENT. In this site's new system, CONFIRMED maps to Confirmed, PROBABLE maps to Supported, ALLEGED maps to Contested (a claim resting on a single source that is not independently verified, shown with its range, never as a settled fact), and CONFIRMED ABSENT maps to a gap entry in "What we do not know", with the absence itself graded Confirmed where the search across institutional channels is documented.
  4. Ranges, not picked numbers. Where published series disagree, the range and the definitions are published.
  5. Public data only. Published bulletins, datasets, archives, satellite imagery, and secondary sources. Gaps are stated, not filled by unpublished gathering. No RTI, interview, or fieldwork is used in this programme.
  6. Satellite corroboration, not proof. Imagery shows proximity and change. It does not measure soil or blood lead, and every satellite claim needs a sensor, date, resolution, cloud cover, baseline, and ground corroboration.
  7. Human final. AI may audit — flag claims without a source, check arithmetic, find outliers. It may not originate a claim, a number, or a causal explanation, and unpublished or source-identifying material is never put into a hosted AI tool.
  8. Corrections on the story. A correction appears dated on the affected page and in the log.
  9. Visuals. One idea per chart, honest axes, direct labels (never colour alone), a full data table under every chart, alt text on every image, and a stated date, cloud cover, and baseline for every satellite pair. A chart that only works as an image is not publishable. A map is never served whole at 8.77 MB to a phone reader; maps are simplified, with a data table underneath.
  10. Safety. Aggregation, not case-level tables, where re-identification is possible; jigsaw identification checks, especially for rape-justice and any vulnerable person; small-cell suppression; trauma-informed review before publication of high-risk stories.
