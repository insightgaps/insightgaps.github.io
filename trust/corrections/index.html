Corrections Policy & Log

Corrections are a core credibility feature, not an institutional embarrassment. When a calculation is erroneous, a denominator is incomplete, or a source dataset is revised, we amend the finding transparently.

Our Corrections Policy

We adhere to strict transparency protocols whenever errors or omissions occur:

  1. Dual Disclosure: Every correction appears on the affected investigation page directly beneath the headline and is permanently entered into this chronological log.
  2. Append-Only Integrity: Entries in this log are never overwritten, edited, or removed.
  3. Mathematical Specificity: Every correction states the previous figure, the amended figure, the cause of the discrepancy, and the date the revision was committed.
  4. Submitting a Correction: Readers, official agencies, and researchers who identify mathematical or factual errors are invited to submit documented errata via our contact page.

Chronological Corrections Log

C-005 · 2026-09-03 · The Lead Belt (BD-INV-003)

Spatial and count reconciliation across report and data payload

Several published figures were internally inconsistent with the report's own embedded dataset: the "Satellite Active" filter chip said 26 while the data flags 19 high-confidence sites; the critical-areas label did not distinguish 68 critical sites from the 65 exceeding 100,000 ppm; the headline stated "507 upazila boundaries" while the embedded map payload contains 93 upazila polygons; the choropleth's student model (400 students per school) summed to 58,000 while the headline used 275 per school (39,875); and the 39,875 figure was described as "children at extreme risk" rather than as a projection of exposure instances.

Amended: Labels reconciled ("Satellite confirmed: 26 (high-confidence: 19)"; "Critical: 68 · 65 exceed 100,000 ppm"; upazila count corrected to 93; choropleth regenerated at ×275, summing to 39,875; headline reframed as about 39,875 exposure instances, unique-school estimate about 33,275, audited range 24,650–50,750); 59 m closest-school claim scoped to featured site; provenance note added documenting snapshot-replication discrepancy (166 / 51 / 125 versus 145 / 44 / 121); manifest date corrected to 2026-05-25.

C-004 · 2026-09-03 · The Impunity Machine (BD-INV-002)

Source registry inventory reconciliation

The published source count of 91 named sources overstated the archived source registry, which contains 65 entries (54 Confirmed, 11 Supported). 27 IDs in the S-01 to S-91 sequence had no registry entries.

Amended: Source count corrected to 65 across report, detailed report, methodology page, manifest, and evidence page; S-26 re-mapped to registry entry S-37 (UNFPA/BBS VAW Survey 2024 Highlights); S-27 and S-28 parenthetical citations removed, source names retained without dead IDs.

C-003 · 2026-09-03 · The Impunity Machine (BD-INV-002)

Disposal rate versus conviction rate denominator disclosure

A note disclosing that the Bangladesh 0.46% conviction rate and the UK, India, and South Africa comparator rates use different denominators (all complaints registered at One-Stop Crisis Centres versus prosecuted trials in court) was added to the comparison section after initial publication.

Amended: Comparison chart now carries the denominator note; the comparison must not be read as like-for-like conviction rates. The 53.52% CEDAW figure is labelled strictly as a case disposal rate, never as a conviction rate.

C-002 · 2026-09-03 · Blood Routes (BD-INV-001)

Eid-ul-Adha year-on-year surge percentage arithmetic

The Eid-ul-Adha 2025 timeline entry described a "29% year-on-year surge" in road fatalities. The report's own timeline data (312 deaths in 2025 vs 262 in 2024) yields a 19.08% increase, not 29%.

Amended: Timeline description corrected to "Sharp 19% year-on-year rise (312 vs 262 in 2024)", matching audited arithmetic.

C-001 · 2026-06-08 · Blood Routes (BD-INV-001)

Night-time crash percentage retraction and recalculation

The report's night-time crash statistic was published as 97.13% (derived from an academic repository dataset later found to contain about 95% synthetic padding); it was corrected to 41.56% (Road Safety Foundation Dhaka Annual Report 2025 basis) on 2026-06-08, after the pipeline retracted the original figure on 2026-06-03.

Amended: Night-time share of fatal Dhaka crashes corrected from 97.13% to 41.56% (10pm–6am window, audited RSF basis).
